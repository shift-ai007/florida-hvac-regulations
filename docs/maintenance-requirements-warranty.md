# Florida HVAC Maintenance Requirements for Warranty Compliance

One of the most misunderstood aspects of HVAC ownership in Florida is the relationship between routine maintenance and warranty coverage. Manufacturers and contractors have specific requirements that, if not met, can void warranty coverage — leaving homeowners with thousands in out-of-pocket costs.

This guide explains what Florida homeowners need to know about maintaining warranty compliance for their AC system.

## The Annual Maintenance Requirement

Almost every HVAC manufacturer requires **at least one professional maintenance inspection per year** to keep the equipment warranty valid. For Florida homes, where the system runs 8–10 months out of the year, many manufacturers now require **two inspections per year**.

> A licensed Florida AC contractor can perform these inspections and provide the documentation you need. Schedule your [AC maintenance](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-maintenance/) visit and keep the invoice as proof of compliance.

### What the Annual Inspection Must Cover

For a maintenance visit to count toward warranty compliance, it must go beyond a simple filter change:

| Component | What Must Be Verified |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Air filter | Checked and replaced if needed |
| Evaporator coil | Visually inspected for cleanliness and frost |
| Condenser coil | Cleaned and inspected for damage |
| Condensate drain | Flushed and verified clear |
| Refrigerant charge | Measured and compared to manufacturer spec |
| Temperature split | Measured (return vs supply) |
| Electrical connections | Tightened and inspected for corrosion |
| Capacitors | Tested for microfarad rating within spec |
| Fan motors | Amp draw measured and compared to nameplate |
| Safety controls | Verified functional (float switch, high-pressure switch) |
| Thermostat | Calibrated and operation verified |

A contractor who does not include refrigerant charge verification and electrical testing may not provide the documentation a manufacturer demands.

## Florida-Specific Conditions Affecting Warranty

### Coastal Corrosion Exclusions

Most major manufacturers (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman, Daikin) include a **coastal corrosion exclusion** in their warranty terms. Damage caused by salt-air corrosion is **not covered** by the standard warranty.

**How to protect yourself:**
- If within 5 miles of the Florida coast, ask about the manufacturer's coastal warranty upgrade
- Regular coil rinsing and anti-corrosion treatments, documented by a licensed contractor, may support a goodwill claim
- Timely [AC maintenance](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-maintenance/) with documented coastal mitigation improves warranty adjustment odds

### Registration Deadlines

Most manufacturers give **60–90 days from installation** to register the product warranty. Miss it and the 10-year parts warranty drops to 5 years automatically.

**Florida gotchas:**
- Seasonal homeowners may miss registration notices sent to a primary address
- If the contractor never registered the system, warranty starts from their purchase date, not installation date
- Verify registration by calling the manufacturer with model and serial numbers immediately after installation

### Proof of Proper Sizing

Florida building code requires a Manual J load calculation for new installations. An oversized system gives manufacturers grounds to deny warranty coverage for short-cycling damage and compressor failure.

## Documentation You Must Keep

| Document | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| Installation contract with model and serial numbers | Proves equipment identity and installation date |
| Load calculation (Manual J) | Proves proper sizing |
| Professional maintenance invoices (annual) | Proves inspection requirement was met |
| Filter purchase receipts | Shows proactive maintenance |
| Repair invoices | Documents prompt attention to issues |
| Warranty registration confirmation | Proves 60–90 day deadline was met |

### What a Maintenance Invoice Must Show

A "tune-up" receipt saying "checked system and found OK" is insufficient. Your invoice should include:
- Date of service
- Contractor name and Florida license number (CAC or CMC)
- System make and model number
- Specific items checked
- Refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling measurements
- Temperature split before and after service
- Capacitor and amp draw readings
- Parts replaced or adjustments made
- Technician's name and signature

## Homeowner Responsibilities

- **Change air filters every 1–3 months** — more often in summer or with pets. Documented filter changes support warranty claims.
- **Keep outdoor unit clear** — 2 feet of clearance on all sides. Vegetation encroachment can void coverage.
- **Address unusual symptoms promptly** — documentation that you called a professional at the first sign strengthens your warranty position.
- **Maintain proper airflow** — do not close more than 20% of supply registers. Restricted airflow can void coverage.

## Dealing with a Denied Warranty Claim

### Florida's Construction Defect Statute (Chapter 558)
Establishes a right to repair before litigation. If a manufacturer denies based on improper installation (not maintenance), you can demand a meeting with the installing contractor.

### Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA)
If a warranty denial is based on a requirement not disclosed at purchase, FDUTPA may provide grounds to challenge it.

### The 10-Year New Construction Warranty
Florida's building code requires a 10-year warranty for new residential construction HVAC. If the system fails due to improper installation by the original builder, this warranty may apply even after the manufacturer's expires.

### What to Do
1. Request denial in writing with the specific reason
2. Check against the warranty document
3. Provide alternative documentation (filter receipts, photos, calendar logs)
4. Ask about goodwill consideration for coastal corrosion denials
5. For systems under 3 years, contact Florida DBPR
6. Get an independent assessment from a licensed AC contractor
