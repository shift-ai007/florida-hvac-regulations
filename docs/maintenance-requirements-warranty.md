# Florida HVAC Maintenance Requirements for Warranty Compliance

One of the most misunderstood aspects of HVAC ownership in Florida is the relationship between routine maintenance and warranty coverage. Manufacturers and contractors have specific requirements that, if not met, can void warranty coverage — leaving homeowners with thousands in out-of-pocket costs.

This guide explains what Florida homeowners need to know about maintaining warranty compliance for their AC system.

## The Annual Maintenance Requirement

Almost every HVAC manufacturer in the United States requires **at least one professional maintenance inspection per year** to keep the equipment warranty valid. For Florida homes, where the system runs 8–10 months out of the year, many manufacturers recommend — and some now require — **two inspections per year**.

> A licensed Florida AC contractor can perform these inspections and provide the documentation you need. Schedule your [AC maintenance](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-maintenance/) visit and keep the invoice as proof of compliance. Without it, a warranty claim for a failed compressor under 5 years old may be denied.

### What the Annual Inspection Must Cover

For a maintenance visit to count toward warranty compliance, it must go beyond a simple filter change. Most manufacturers require documentation that these items were checked:

| Component | What Must Be Verified |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Air filter | Checked and replaced if needed |
| Evaporator coil | Visually inspected for cleanliness and frost |
| Condenser coil | Cleaned and inspected for damage |
| Condensate drain | Flushed and verified clear |
| Refrigerant charge | Measured and compared to manufacturer spec |
| Temperature split | Measured (return vs supply) |
| Electrical connections | Tightened and inspected for corrosion |
| Capacitors | Tested for microfarad rating within spec |
| Fan motors | Amp draw measured and compared to nameplate |
| Safety controls | Verified functional (float switch, high-pressure switch) |
| Thermostat | Calibrated and operation verified |

This is more than a "tune-up" — it is a documented inspection that creates a record of due diligence. A contractor who does not include refrigerant charge verification and electrical testing in their standard visit may not provide the documentation a manufacturer demands.

## Florida-Specific Conditions That Affect Warranty

Manufacturers know that Florida's climate is harder on AC equipment than most other regions. Their warranty terms reflect this.

### Coastal Corrosion Exclusions

Most major manufacturers (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman, Daikin) include a specific **coastal corrosion exclusion** in their warranty terms. This exclusion says that damage caused by salt-air corrosion is **not covered** by the standard warranty, even if the compressor fails within the first year.

**What the exclusion typically says:**
- Damage from "corrosive environment" including salt spray, chemical exposure, or coastal atmospheric conditions is excluded
- Rusted condenser coils, corroded electrical terminals, and seized compressor from salt ingress are explicitly not covered
- Some manufacturers offer a separate "coastal" or "severe environment" warranty for an additional cost

**How to protect yourself:**
- If you live within 5 miles of the Florida coast, ask about the manufacturer's coastal warranty upgrade when purchasing a new system
- Regular coil rinsing and anti-corrosion treatments, documented by a licensed contractor, may support a goodwill claim even under a standard warranty
- Timely [AC maintenance](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-maintenance/) with documentation of coastal mitigation efforts improves the odds of a warranty adjustment if corrosion damage occurs

### Registration Deadlines

Most manufacturers give you **60–90 days from installation** to register the product warranty online. Miss the deadline and the standard 10-year parts warranty drops to 5 years automatically — with no exception process in most cases.

**Florida-specific gotchas:**
- If your home is a second home or seasonal property, the registration notice may have been sent to your primary address while you were away
- If the contractor installed the system but never registered it (this is more common than it should be), your warranty starts from the day the contractor bought the equipment, not the day the system started cooling your home
- Verify registration status by calling the manufacturer with your unit model and serial numbers immediately after installation

### Proof of Proper Sizing

Florida's building code requires a Manual J load calculation for any new installation. If a system is oversized, manufacturers may deny warranty coverage for short-cycling damage, compressor failure, or inadequate dehumidification. If your contractor did not perform a load calculation before installation, your warranty may be compromised from day one.

## Documentation You Must Keep

Manufacturers do not accept verbal claims that maintenance was performed. You need a paper trail.

### Maintain These Records
| Document | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| Installation contract with model and serial numbers | Proves equipment identity and installation date |
| Load calculation (Manual J) | Proves proper sizing if short-cycling claims arise |
| Professional maintenance invoices (annual) | Proves annual inspection requirement was met |
| Filter purchase receipts | Shows proactive maintenance between professional visits |
| Repair invoices | Documents prompt attention to issues |
| Warranty registration confirmation | Proves registration within the 60–90 day window |
| Manufacturer's warranty document | States terms you must follow to maintain coverage |

### What a Maintenance Invoice Must Show

A generic "tune-up" receipt that says "checked system and found OK" is not sufficient for a warranty claim. Your maintenance invoice should include:

- Date of service
- Contractor name and Florida license number (CAC or CMC)
- System make and model number
- Specific items checked (from the table above)
- Refrigerant type, pressures, and superheat/subcooling measurements
- Temperature split before and after service
- Capacitor and amp draw readings
- Any parts replaced or adjustments made
- Technician's name and signature

If your current contractor does not provide this level of detail, ask them to upgrade their service ticket. Most will oblige — they understand that warranty claims require documentation.

## Homeowner Responsibilities

Even with professional maintenance, manufacturers expect homeowners to perform basic care:

- **Change air filters every 1–3 months** — more often during peak summer or if you have pets. A documented filter change log (photos or a simple calendar) supports warranty claims.
- **Keep outdoor unit clear** — maintain 2 feet of clearance on all sides. Vegetation encroachment that causes high-head-pressure damage or condenser failure can be used to deny a warranty claim.
- **Promptly address unusual symptoms** — a noise or performance change that is ignored for months, and that a technician later determines caused secondary damage, weakens your warranty position. Documentation that you called a professional when the first sign of trouble appeared strengthens it.
- **Maintain proper airflow** — do not close more than 20% of supply registers. Restricted airflow that leads to a frozen coil and liquid slugging can void coverage.

## Dealing with a Denied Warranty Claim

If a warranty claim is denied, Florida law provides some homeowner protections:

### Florida's Construction Defect Statute (Chapter 558)
While primarily for new construction, this statute establishes a right to repair before litigation. If a manufacturer denies a claim based on improper installation (not maintenance), you have the right to demand a meeting with the installing contractor to discuss repair options before pursuing legal action.

### Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA)
If a manufacturer denies a warranty claim based on a requirement that was not disclosed at the time of purchase — for example, if the warranty document mentions an annual inspection requirement but the sales contract and installation paperwork did not — FDUTPA may provide grounds for challenging the denial.

### The 10-Year Warranty for New Construction
Florida's building code requires a 10-year warranty for new residential construction, including HVAC systems installed as part of the original build. This is a structural warranty administered by the builder, not the equipment manufacturer. If the system fails due to improper installation by the original builder — even if the manufacturer's equipment warranty has expired — the builder's warranty may apply.

### What to Do
1. Request the denial in writing with the specific reason cited
2. Check the cited reason against the warranty document
3. If the denial is based on missing maintenance records, provide alternative documentation (filter receipts, dated photos of the system, calendar logs)
4. If the denial is based on coastal corrosion, ask about goodwill consideration — some manufacturers have internal programs for borderline cases
5. For systems under 3 years old, contact the Florida DBPR (Department of Business and Professional Regulation) — they can investigate whether improper installation by the contractor contributed to the failure
6. If all else fails, a licensed Florida AC contractor can provide an independent assessment of what caused the failure and whether the denial was reasonable

## Summary

| Requirement | What to Do | Risk of Non-Compliance |
|-------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Professional maintenance (annual minimum) | Schedule and document yearly | Denied compressor/parts claims |
| Warranty registration (60–90 days) | Register online immediately after install | 10-year drops to 5-year automatically |
| Coastal corrosion exclusion | Use coastal protection measures, document mitigation | Complete denial for salt-damaged equipment |
| Proper sizing (Manual J) | Verify before installation | Denied short-cycling and dehumidification claims |
| Filter changes (regular) | Change monthly in summer, document schedule | Denied airflow-related failures |
| Prompt issue response | Call a technician at first sign of trouble | Claims of "neglect" if damage is severe |

The Florida HVAC warranty landscape favors homeowners who document everything. A folder with your installation paperwork, annual maintenance invoices, and filter change log is the single most effective investment you can make in protecting your AC investment.
