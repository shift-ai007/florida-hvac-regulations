# HVAC Contract Disclosures: What Florida Law Requires

When you hire an HVAC contractor in Florida, the contract you sign is a binding legal document. Florida law requires specific disclosures in all home improvement contracts — and several more that apply specifically to HVAC work. Understanding what must be in your contract and what should raise red flags protects you from unlicensed contractors, lien issues, and costly misunderstandings.

## Florida's Home Improvement Contract Requirements

Florida Statute 489.119 requires all home improvement contracts over $1,000 to be in writing and include:

### Mandatory Elements

**1. Complete Contractor Information**
- Full legal name and business name (exactly as registered with the Florida DBPR)
- Business address (a PO Box alone is not sufficient — a physical address is required)
- Florida license number (CAC, CMC, or RA — must match the classification of the work being performed)
- Telephone number

**2. Detailed Scope of Work**
A clear description of all work to be performed, including:
- Make, model, and capacity of all equipment being installed
- Brand, type, and specifications of materials and ductwork
- The scope of what IS included (permits, disposal of old equipment, duct modification, etc.)
- A list of what is NOT included (electrical work, sheetrock repair, painting, etc.)

**3. Total Price and Payment Schedule**
- The complete contract price, including all taxes, fees, and permit costs
- Any finance charges or interest if financing is involved
- The payment schedule, including deposit amount and milestone payments
- **Florida law limits deposits to 10% or $1,000, whichever is less**, for new residential systems. Some municipalities have stricter limits.

**4. Commencement and Completion Dates**
- Projected start date and completion date
- Circumstances that would allow delays (weather, permit delays, material availability)

**5. Warranty Information**
- Manufacturer's warranty duration and what it covers (parts only or parts and labor)
- Contractor's labor warranty (the industry standard in Florida is 1 year minimum)
- Any exclusions or conditions that void the warranty
- Whether the warranty is transferable if you sell the home

**6. Right to Cancel**
- Florida law provides a 3-business-day cooling-off period for contracts signed in your home
- The contract must include a cancellation notice in at least 10-point bold type
- If you cancel within 3 days, the contractor must return all payments within 10 days

### Required Contractor License Disclosures

Florida HVAC contractors must include their license number on ALL:
- Written contracts
- Estimates and proposals
- Advertisements (including websites)
- Vehicle signage

**Before signing**, verify the license is current and in good standing at the [Florida DBPR License Search](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp). If the license number on the contract doesn't match the DBPR database, do not sign — it may be someone else's number being used illegally.

## Red Flags in HVAC Contracts

### Vague Language

- "All necessary materials" — Does not specify brands, models, or capacities
- "Standard installation" — Does not define what standard includes
- "Permits if required" — Permits are always required for replacements and new installations. The contractor should confirm they will obtain them.
- "Estimated completion" — Without a definition of what counts as a valid delay

### Concerning Payment Terms

- Any request for more than 10% down (unless it's a very large commercial project)
- Payment due "upon arrival of equipment" — Before installation
- Final payment due before final inspection passes
- Cash-only payment terms
- Payments to be made to an individual, not the company

### License Red Flags

- The company name on the contract doesn't match the name on the license
- The license on the contract belongs to a different trade classification (e.g., a general contractor license for HVAC work)
- The license is listed as "pending" or "inactive" on DBPR
- The contractor can't produce a physical business card or their name doesn't appear in the DBPR system as a qualifying agent

## Florida's Construction Lien Law Notice

Florida Statute 713.015 requires a specific notice in all home improvement contracts over $2,500. Look for wording substantially similar to:

> **Florida's Construction Lien Law**
>
> According to Florida's Construction Lien Law (Sections 713.001-713.37, Florida Statutes), those who work on your property or provide materials and services are entitled to file a construction lien if they are not paid. If your contractor fails to pay subcontractors or material suppliers, those parties may file a lien against your property and sue to foreclose. This can result in your property being sold to satisfy the debt. To protect yourself, you should make all payments directly to the contractor, and before making final payment, you should require the contractor to provide you with a written release of lien from every person or company that provided labor, services, or materials.

Failure to include this notice makes the contract legally voidable. If you don't see this notice in your contract, you have the right to cancel without penalty within 3 days of receiving it.

## Florida HVAC Contract Addendums

### Mold Disclosure (Florida Statute 468.84)

If the work includes cutting into existing ductwork, walls, or ceilings — which most replacement installations do — the contractor must provide a mold disclosure notice:

- Informs you that mold may be present in existing construction and that disturbing it could release mold spores
- Allows you to decide whether to have mold testing performed before work begins
- Acknowledges that the contractor is not responsible for mold remediation unless specifically contracted

### Workers' Compensation Disclosure

If the contractor has fewer than 4 employees (common for small HVAC businesses), they may not carry workers' compensation insurance. Florida law requires a specific disclosure if this is the case:

- The contract must state in bold type that the contractor is not required to carry workers' compensation
- This means you, the homeowner, could be financially responsible if a worker is injured on your property
- If this disclosure is absent but the contractor doesn't have coverage, they are acting illegally

### Financing Disclosures

If you're financing the HVAC system:

- The APR, loan term, and total finance charge must be disclosed in a separate Truth in Lending statement
- Retail installment sales contracts have specific Florida requirements
- Never sign a blank or incomplete financing agreement

## Your Rights Before, During, and After Installation

### Before Work Starts

- You have the right to see proof of insurance (general liability and workers' compensation)
- You have the right to verify the permit was pulled (get the permit number)
- You can request at least 24 hours' notice before work begins
- You can schedule a pre-installation walk-through to document existing conditions

### During the Job

- You have the right to reasonable progress updates
- You can withhold payment proportional to incomplete work
- You have the right to approve change orders in writing before extra charges apply
- You can request a written explanation for any delay

### After Installation

- You have the right to a final inspection pass (if the permit requires one)
- You have the right to a Certificate of Occupancy or Certificate of Completion from the building department
- You can request a final walk-through and demonstration of the system operation
- You have the right to copies of all manufacturer warranty registration documents
- You have the right to a release of lien from the contractor and all subcontractors

## Questions to Ask Before Signing

1. "Who will be performing the work?" (company employees or subcontractors?)
2. "Will you pull the permit, or do I need to arrange that?" (they should pull it)
3. "What brand, model, and SEER rating is quoted?" (get specific model numbers)
4. "What is included in the labor warranty?" (parts, labor, travel?)
5. "What happens if the installation runs longer than estimated?" (daily rate for delays?)
6. "Do you have general liability and workers' compensation insurance?" (ask for proof)
7. "Who do I call if there's a problem after installation?" (24-hour emergency line?)
8. "Can you provide references from recent similar installations?" (and call them)
