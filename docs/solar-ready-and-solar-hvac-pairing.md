# Solar-Ready Requirements & Solar HVAC Pairing in Florida

Florida's combination of abundant sunshine and year-round cooling demand makes it the perfect market for solar-powered HVAC. As of 2026, new Florida building codes require solar-ready provisions for most residential construction, and pairing solar PV with heat pump systems is emerging as one of the most cost-effective energy strategies in the state.

This guide covers code requirements, system design, incentive stacking, and practical considerations for homeowners and contractors.

## Solar-Ready Code Requirements (2023 FBC)

The 2023 Florida Building Code — Energy Conservation Chapter requires **solar-ready zones** on new residential construction. These provisions don't require solar panels, but they mandate that the roof and electrical system are designed to accept future solar installation.

### Applicability

Applies to **new residential buildings** with:
- Roof area ≥ 600 sq ft of solar-ready zone available
- Unshaded roof area oriented between 110° and 270° of true north (roughly east through south to west)

**Exemptions**:
- Buildings where the solar-ready zone is entirely shaded by existing buildings, terrain, or permanent structures
- Buildings with a permanently installed solar PV or solar thermal system of at least 0.5 W/sq ft per roof area
- Additions, alterations, and repairs to existing buildings

### Requirements

| Component | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Solar-ready zone area** | Minimum 300 sq ft of contiguous roof area. On sloped roofs, at least 50% must be oriented between 110° and 270° (south-facing preferred). |
| **Documentation** | A solar-ready zone diagram must be included in the construction drawings showing: zone location, dimensions, structural load capacity, and electrical pathway. |
| **Structural capacity** | The solar-ready zone must be designed to support an additional 6 psf (pounds per square foot) of live load — sufficient for standard solar panel arrays. |
| **Conduit pathway** | A pathway for conduit from the solar-ready zone to the electrical panel must be identified and reserved. This includes a labeled route through framing members. |
| **Electrical panel** | The main electrical panel must have a reserved breaker space labeled "Future Solar" adjacent to or within the panel. The busbar must be rated to handle the future solar backfeed. |
| **Interconnection documentation** | Plans must note: "Future solar PV system interconnection is permitted per NEC 705 and Florida building code section R401.7." |

### Penalties for Non-Compliance

Building departments across Florida — particularly in Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach — are enforcing the solar-ready provisions. Non-compliant plans are rejected at permit application. Retroactive compliance can cost $2,000-5,000 in structural and electrical modifications.

## Solar + HVAC: The Strategic Pairing

### Why Solar and Heat Pumps Are Natural Partners

A heat pump is essentially an air conditioner that can run in reverse. In cooling mode, it works identically to a standard AC. In heating mode, it extracts heat from outside air and moves it indoors — a process that's 200-400% efficient (compared to 80-95% for a gas furnace).

The strategic value of pairing solar with a heat pump:

| Metric | Solar + Standard AC | Solar + Heat Pump |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Cooling offset** | 60-80% of cooling load | 60-80% of cooling load |
| **Heating offset** | 0% (gas/electric strip heat) | 60-80% of heating load |
| **Year-round solar utilization** | ~6 months (cooling season only) | ~12 months (cooling + heating + DHW) |
| **Net metering payout** | Often over-generates in spring = low-value credit | Better year-round load match = higher self-consumption |
| **ROI on solar** | 7-10 years (partial utilization) | 4-7 years (full utilization) |

### The FPL Rate Structure Factor

Florida Power & Light and most Florida utilities use **net metering** with:
- **Full retail credit** for excess kWh (up to your system size cap)
- **Monthly netting** (generation offsets consumption within each billing cycle)
- **Annual true-up** at the end of each 12-month period

This means that over-generating in the winter (solar producing more than you consume) earns you retail credit that you can use in the summer. A heat pump increases winter consumption, minimizing the over-generation gap and maximizing the value of your solar array.

## System Sizing

### Rule of Thumb

For a typical South Florida home:

| Home Size | AC Size | Solar PV Needed (AC-only offset) | Solar PV Needed (HP + full offset) |
|-----------|---------|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| 1,500 sq ft | 3 tons | 5-6 kW | 7-9 kW |
| 2,000 sq ft | 4 tons | 6-8 kW | 9-12 kW |
| 2,500 sq ft | 5 tons | 8-10 kW | 12-15 kW |
| 3,000 sq ft | 5-6 tons | 10-12 kW | 14-18 kW |

### The Self-Consumption Sweet Spot

The most economical solar + heat pump configuration is sized to **cover 80-90% of annual consumption** — leaving a small grid draw during peak winter evenings. Going to 100%+ adds battery cost or produces low-value net metering credits. The optimal ratio is approximately **1.2 kW of solar per ton of heat pump capacity** in South Florida.

## Incentive Stacking (2026)

Florida homeowners can stack multiple incentives for solar + efficient HVAC:

| Incentive | Type | Value | Eligibility |
|-----------|------|-------|-------------|
| **Federal Solar ITC** | Tax credit (IRA 25D) | 30% of solar PV cost, no cap | Primary + secondary residences. Equipment must be placed in service by 2033 (steps down to 26% in 2034, 22% in 2035). |
| **Federal Heat Pump Tax Credit** | Tax credit (IRA 25C) | 30% of heat pump cost, up to $2,000/year | Must meet CEE highest-tier efficiency. Primary residence only. |
| **HEEHRA Rebate (FL)** | Point-of-sale rebate | Up to $8,000 for heat pump, up to $14,000 total for whole-home | Income-limited (<150% AMI). Administered by the Florida Department of Agriculture. Program launch anticipated late 2026. |
| **FPL Solar Rebate** | Rebate (if available) | $0.10-0.20 per watt, up to $1,000 | FPL residential customers. Subject to program funding availability. |
| **Net Metering** | Ongoing savings | Full retail rate for excess generation | All Florida IOUs (FPL, Duke, TECO) offer NEM per Florida statute 366.91. |

### Stacking Example — Typical South Florida Home

A 2,000 sq ft home installing a 10 kW solar array ($22,000) and a 4-ton heat pump ($8,000 installed):

1. Solar ITC (30% of $22,000) = -$6,600
2. Heat pump 25C credit (30% of $8,000, capped at $2,000) = -$2,000
3. HEEHRA heat pump rebate (if income-qualified) = -$8,000
4. Net cost: $22,000 + $8,000 - $6,600 - $2,000 - $8,000 = **$13,400**
5. Annual energy savings: $1,800-2,400
6. **Simple payback: 5.5-7.5 years**

Without HEEHRA, net cost is $21,400 with a payback of 9-12 years.

## Interconnection & Permitting in Florida

### Solar + HVAC Permit Requirements

| Component | Building Permit | Electrical Permit | Mechanical Permit | HOA Approval |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Solar PV array | Yes (structural) | Yes | No | Yes (per Ch. 720) |
| Heat pump replacement | Yes (if new location) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Main panel upgrade | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Battery storage | Yes (structural + fire) | Yes | No | Yes |

### Florida-Specific Solar Permitting Nuances

- **Miami-Dade**: Requires NOA (Notice of Acceptance) for all solar equipment and racking. Solar PV must use Miami-Dade approved modules and inverters. See [Miami-Dade NOA requirements](docs/miami-dade-noa-requirements.md).
- **Broward**: Unified permitting through the Broward County OneStop portal. No county-specific solar approval beyond standard building code.
- **Palm Beach**: Multiple municipal jurisdictions with varying solar permit fees ($150-500). Some municipalities require structural engineering review for roof-mounted arrays.
- **FPL territory**: Net metering application must be submitted separately from building permits. FPL requires an interconnection agreement before activating the system.

### The Interconnection Process

1. **Design and permit** — Contractor submits solar + HVAC plans to local building department
2. **Install** — Solar array, heat pump, and electrical work completed
3. **Inspection** — Local building inspector signs off on all permits
4. **FPL interconnection application** — Submitted with permit sign-off, single-line diagram, equipment spec sheets
5. **FPL meter exchange** — FPL installs a bi-directional meter (typically 5-15 business days after application approval)
6. **PTO** — Permission to Operate granted. System can be turned on.

Total timeline: 6-16 weeks from permit application to PTO.

## Equipment Considerations

### Solar-Ready vs Solar-Optimized

Not all AC and heat pump systems work well with solar. Key differences:

| Feature | Standard | Solar-Optimized |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| **Inverter type** | Single-stage or two-stage | Variable-speed (inverter-driven) |
| **Power consumption** | Fixed or two-step | Continuously variable, ramps down with solar |
| **DC coupling** | No | Optional — can run directly from DC solar |
| **Smart grid ready** | No | Yes — CT-clamp monitoring adjusts load to match solar generation |
| **Premium over standard** | — | $1,500-3,000 |

Variable-speed heat pumps and air conditioners are strongly recommended for solar pairing because they can ramp down to match available solar generation — consuming only 0.5-1.5 kW at low speed instead of cycling on and off at 3-5 kW. This dramatically improves self-consumption.

### Battery Storage

Batteries are optional but increasingly cost-effective in Florida:
- **Without battery**: Solar powers the AC when the sun is shining; grid powers it at night. Net metering handles the swap.
- **With battery (5-10 kWh)**: Solar charges battery during the day; battery powers AC through peak evening hours (5-8 PM) when FPL time-of-use rates are highest. Adds $6,000-12,000 to system cost but can reduce payback by 2-3 years in TOU scenarios.
- **Whole-home battery (10-20 kWh)**: Full backup capability. Powers AC, refrigerator, lights, and well pump during outages. Adds $12,000-20,000.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Oversizing solar to compensate for an inefficient AC** — You'll need 30-50% more solar panels than if you upgraded the HVAC first. The solar ITC applies to the full system cost, but the heat pump 25C credit is capped at $2,000. It's almost always better to upgrade the AC first, then size the solar for the reduced load.
2. **Installing the heat pump before checking solar-ready panel capacity** — A heat pump with auxiliary (electric strip) heat can draw 8-15 kW on startup. If your electrical panel doesn't have capacity, you may need a $1,500-3,000 panel upgrade before solar can be interconnected.
3. **Ignoring HOA covenants** — Many Florida HOAs restrict roof-mounted solar visibility. Chapter 720 limits HOA power to prohibit solar, but restrictions on placement (e.g., front-facing roof only) are still enforceable. Obtain written HOA approval before signing a solar contract.
4. **Choosing a single-stage AC to save money on the heat pump** — A single-stage compressor paired with solar only runs at 100% power or 0%. You lose the self-consumption benefits that make the solar/HVAC pairing economical. The $1,500-2,000 upgrade to variable-speed pays for itself in 2-3 years through better solar utilization.
5. **Not planning for future battery storage** — Even if you're not installing batteries now, ensure your inverter is battery-ready (hybrid inverter with backup port). Retrofitting battery capability later costs $1,000-2,000 more than buying the right inverter upfront.
