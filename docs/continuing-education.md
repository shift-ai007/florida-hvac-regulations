# Continuing Education Requirements for Florida HVAC Contractors

Florida HVAC contractors must complete continuing education (CE) hours to maintain their license. This ensures contractors stay current with code changes, safety practices, and new technology.

## Requirements by License Type

### CAC (Class A Air Conditioning)

- **Renewal cycle**: Every 2 years (odd-numbered years, by August 31)
- **Total CE hours required**: 14 hours per cycle
- **Breakdown**:
  - 1 hour: Workers compensation
  - 1 hour: Workplace safety (OSHA)
  - 1 hour: Business practices
  - 1 hour: Florida Building Code update
  - 10 hours: Technical/elective courses

### CMC (Mechanical Contractor)

- **Renewal cycle**: Every 2 years (same as CAC)
- **Total CE hours required**: 14 hours per cycle
- **Same breakdown as CAC** with additional emphasis on commercial code updates

## Approved CE Providers

CE courses must be approved by the Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB). Common providers:

| Provider | Format | Cost Range |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| FACCA (FL Air Conditioning Contractors Assoc.) | In-person, online | $75-$200 |
| ACCA National | Online, conference | $100-$300 |
| Gold Coast Schools | Online, classroom | $50-$175 |
| Building Officials Training Institute | In-person | $100-$250 |
| Various online CE providers | Self-paced online | $50-$150 |

## What CE Courses Cover

### Required Topics

**Florida Building Code Updates**: Every code cycle (every 3 years), contractors must learn the new requirements. The 8th Edition FBC (2023) brought significant HVAC changes including updated energy code and efficiency standards.

**Workers Compensation**: Florida workers comp laws, requirements, exemptions, and recent changes.

**Workplace Safety**: OSHA standards applicable to HVAC work, fall protection, electrical safety, refrigerant handling safety.

**Business Practices**: Contract law, lien law updates, proper business documentation, insurance requirements.

### Common Elective Topics

- New refrigerant technologies (R-454B, R-32)
- Manual J/S/D load calculation updates
- Indoor air quality and ventilation standards
- Duct leakage testing methods
- Smart HVAC controls and building automation
- High-efficiency system design
- Humidity control in hot-humid climates
- Solar-assisted HVAC systems

## Consequences of Non-Compliance

If CE requirements aren't met by the renewal deadline:

1. **Delinquent status**: License becomes delinquent after expiration
2. **Grace period**: Typically 1-2 months to complete CE and pay late fees
3. **Late fees**: Additional $50-$100 per month
4. **Null and void**: After extended delinquency, license goes null and void
5. **Reinstatement**: Requires completing all missed CE, paying accumulated fees, and potentially re-examining

## How Homeowners Can Use This Information

When verifying a contractor:
- Ask when their license was last renewed
- A recently renewed license means current CE is complete
- Check [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp) - the renewal date is shown
- A contractor who maintains CE is more likely to know current code requirements

## Renewal Process

1. Complete all 14 CE hours before August 31 of renewal year
2. Log in to DBPR online portal
3. Affirm CE completion (keep certificates for 4 years)
4. Pay renewal fee (~$209 for CAC)
5. License status updates to "Current, Active" for next 2-year cycle

**Tip for contractors**: Don't wait until the last month. CE providers get overwhelmed near deadlines, and some courses fill up. Complete CE early in the renewal cycle.
