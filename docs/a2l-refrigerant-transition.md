# A2L Refrigerant Transition — 2026 Florida Code Update

The HVAC industry is undergoing its biggest refrigerant transition since the R-22 phaseout. As of 2026, new AC systems must use **A2L classified refrigerants** (mildly flammable) instead of the R-410A that has been standard for the past 15 years. This document covers what Florida homeowners and contractors need to know.

## What Changed and Why

The AIM (American Innovation and Manufacturing) Act of 2020 authorized the EPA to phase down HFC refrigerants. R-410A has a Global Warming Potential (GWP) of 2,088. The new A2L refrigerants — primarily **R-32** and **R-454B** — have a GWP of 675 (R-32) or 466 (R-454B), roughly 70-78% lower.

### Timeline

| Date | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| January 2025 | EPA finalized the technology transition rule |
| 2025-2027 | Manufacturer transition period — most brands ship R-454B or R-32 |
| January 2026 | New systems sold must comply (most manufacturers started 2025) |
| 2026-2032 | R-410A production being phased down in stepped reductions |

## What This Means for Florida Homeowners

### Existing R-410A Systems

- **No forced replacement** — your current R-410A system is fine. There is no requirement to replace a working system.
- **R-410A still available for service** — reclaimed and stockpiled R-410A will be available for repairs for years. It will become progressively more expensive as supply dwindles.
- **Leak repairs still work** — small refrigerant leaks in existing systems can still be repaired and recharged with R-410A.

### New Installations (2026+)

If you're buying a new AC system in 2026:
- The unit will use **R-454B or R-32** — both are A2L mildly flammable refrigerants
- The system is designed specifically for the new refrigerant (not a drop-in retrofit)
- Equipment costs are similar to R-410A systems — refrigerant cost may vary
- Efficiency ratings (SEER2) continue to improve with new compressor technology

## Florida-Specific Regulatory Impact

### Florida Building Code (FBC 2026)

The 2026 Florida Building Code (adopted from 2024 IECC with Florida amendments) includes new requirements for A2L refrigerants:

1. **Mechanical ventilation**: Indoor units with A2L refrigerant require mechanical ventilation or proximity alarms in certain configurations
2. **Setback distances**: Minimum distances from windows, doors, and building openings for outdoor units using A2L refrigerants
3. **Detection systems**: In some configurations (equipment installed in occupied spaces below grade or without windows), refrigerant detection sensors may be required
4. **Labeling requirements**: All A2L systems must have visible warning labels indicating the refrigerant type and flammability class

### Licensing Implications

Standard Florida contractor licenses (CAC, CMC) cover A2L system installation. No separate licensing is required for A2L refrigerants, but:

- Technicians must complete EPA Section 608 **Type I or Universal certification** that covers low-GWP refrigerants
- Additional manufacturer-specific training is recommended (most brands offer free online training)
- Recovery/recycling equipment must handle A2L-rated pressures

### Safety Standards

A2L refrigerants are classified as **mildly flammable** (ISO 817 Class 2L). In practice:

- They are **difficult to ignite** — requires a direct flame or hot surface above 800°F
- They **burn very slowly** — flame propagation is less than 4 inches per second
- Open-flame sources in mechanical rooms (pilot lights, unvented heaters) must be addressed
- Standard service practices with added ventilation are sufficient for most residential work

## Practical Considerations

### Price Impact

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Equipment cost | Comparable to R-410A — +-5% |
| Installation cost | Slightly higher (ventilation, detector requirements in some cases) |
| Service cost | Similar — refrigerant cost may be slightly higher |
| Long-term cost | R-454B and R-32 are cheaper to produce than R-410A |

### What to Ask Your Contractor

If you're replacing an AC system in 2026:

1. "What refrigerant does this system use — R-454B or R-32?"
2. "Does this installation need any additional ventilation or detectors because of the new refrigerant?"
3. "Is my current R-410A system's indoor coil compatible with the new refrigerant?" (Answer: No — if replacing just the outdoor unit, you need a matched system)
4. "Does this A2L system require any changes to how my equipment closet or attic space is configured?"

## Summary for South Florida Homeowners

- Your existing AC system is fine — there's no recall or forced replacement
- New systems purchased in 2026+ use mildly flammable A2L refrigerants
- These are safe when installed according to code — the flammability risk is extremely low
- R-410A service refrigerant remains available but will cost more over time
- If you're replacing before 2027, R-410A systems are still available and serviceable
