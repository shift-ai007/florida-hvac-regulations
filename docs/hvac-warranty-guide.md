# HVAC Warranty Guide for Florida Homeowners

HVAC equipment is one of the largest investments a homeowner makes. Understanding your warranty coverage — manufacturer and contractor — can save thousands of dollars. This guide explains how HVAC warranties work in Florida, what's covered, and how to make sure you're protected.

## Two Types of Warranty

### Manufacturer's Warranty

Covers defects in the equipment itself — compressor, coils, heat exchanger, and other components. These are provided by the manufacturer (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman, etc.) and are typically:

- **Compressor**: 10 years (standard) or 10-12 years (registered)
- **Parts**: 5-10 years depending on brand and registration
- **Heat exchanger**: 10 years (gas furnaces) to 20 years or lifetime (top-end units)

### Contractor's Labor Warranty

Covers the labor to diagnose and replace a failed part. These are provided by the installing contractor and typically range:

- **Standard**: 1 year parts and labor
- **Premium**: 2-5 years parts and labor
- **Extended**: Up to 10 years with a maintenance agreement

**Important**: A manufacturer may replace a failed compressor for free, but you'll still pay the contractor's labor, refrigerant, and supplies — often $500-1,500.

## Warranty Registration Is Critical

Most manufacturers require **online registration within 60-90 days of installation** to unlock the full warranty. Without registration, the standard warranty typically drops to:

- Compressor: 10 years → 5 years
- Parts: 5-10 years → 1-5 years

**Florida tip**: Many homeowners in South Florida buy vacation or rental properties here. If the AC was installed while the property was unoccupied and the builder or prior owner never registered the equipment, the warranty may be severely reduced. Check registration status before any major repair.

### How to Check Registration Status

1. Locate the model and serial number on the outdoor unit nameplate
2. Visit the manufacturer's warranty lookup page (usually under "Warranty Registration" or "Product Support")
3. Enter the serial number — it will show the registration date, registered owner, and remaining warranty period

For existing homes where you didn't install the equipment, consider paying for a transferable warranty or a home warranty plan that covers HVAC.

## Extended Warranties: Are They Worth It in Florida?

### Manufacturer Extended Warranties (10-12 Year Parts + Labor)

**Pros:**
- Backed by the manufacturer, not a third party
- Transferable to new homeowners (adds resale value)
- Covers both parts and labor
- No deductibles on most plans

**Cons:**
- Must be purchased at installation time (can't add later)
- Typically requires annual professional maintenance to remain valid
- Adds $400-1,200 to the installation cost

**Verdict for South Florida**: Worth it for higher-end systems (16+ SEER, variable-speed, inverter-driven). These systems have more expensive components — a single compressor or variable-speed blower replacement can cost $1,500-3,000. For a basic 14 SEER single-stage system, the math is tighter.

### Home Warranty Plans (Third-Party)

**Pros:**
- Covers multiple systems (AC, heating, plumbing, electrical)
- Can be purchased anytime
- Useful as a safety net for older systems (10+ years)

**Cons:**
- Annual premiums of $500-800 with service call fees of $75-150 per visit
- Coverage limits ($1,500-3,000 max per HVAC component)
- Fine print on refrigerant, coil corrosion, and pre-existing conditions
- Often requires using the warranty company's contractors (variable quality)

**Verdict**: Home warranties can work for older systems nearing end-of-life, but the math usually favors self-insuring by putting the premium money into a repair fund instead.

## Florida-Specific Warranty Considerations

### Saltwater Corrosion

Homes within 3 miles of Florida's coast experience significantly higher condenser coil corrosion rates. Standard manufacturer warranties **do not cover corrosion damage** — it's classified as "environmental damage" or "acts of nature."

**What to do**: If you live in a coastal area (Miami Beach, Fort Lauderdale, Key Biscayne, etc.), look for:
- Units with **epoxy-coated condenser coils** (Carrier's "WeatherShield," Trane's "Spine Fin" with protective coating)
- **Coastal-specific warranties** — some manufacturers offer extended corrosion protection for an additional fee
- **Annual coil cleaning and anti-corrosion treatment** as part of your maintenance plan

### Flood Damage

Standard HVAC warranties explicitly exclude flood damage. With South Florida's hurricane and flood risk:

- Ensure your homeowner's insurance covers flood damage to mechanical systems
- Elevate outdoor units above base flood elevation (BFE) — required for new installations in flood zones
- Consider installing the condensing unit on a raised platform

### Hurricane Damage

Warranty exclusions for hurricane damage are standard. Before hurricane season:
- Secure outdoor units with hurricane-rated straps or brackets
- Document the unit's condition with photos (useful for insurance claims)
- Check your homeowner's policy for HVAC-specific coverage limits

## What Voids an HVAC Warranty

Florida contractors see these warranty-voiding situations regularly:

| Action | Why It Voids Warranty |
|--------|----------------------|
| DIY refrigerant work | EPA regulation + contamination risk |
| Unlicensed contractor service | Most manufacturer policies require licensed techs |
| Missing annual maintenance | Yearly professional inspection required by most 10-year plans |
| Dirty filters causing coil damage | Manufacturer says "lack of maintenance" |
| Oversized or undersized replacement | Unit must match Manual J load calculation |
| Aftermarket refrigerant (R-22 substitutes) | Non-approved refrigerants damage compressors |
| Unregistered equipment | Registration window closed (typically 60-90 days) |

## Questions to Ask Before Any Repair

When a technician recommends replacing a major component or the entire system, ask:

1. "Is this component still under the manufacturer's warranty?" (Have them check by serial number)
2. "What is the labor warranty on this repair?" (Some contractors offer 30-90 days on labor)
3. "Will repairing this void the remaining warranty?" (Replacing a non-warrantied part shouldn't affect others)
4. "Do you offer an extended labor warranty on this repair?" (Some contractors offer 1-2 years on major repairs)
5. "Is there a better OEM part option that extends the warranty?" (OEM parts carry longer warranties than generic)

## Transferring Warranties When Selling Your Home

Florida law does not automatically transfer HVAC warranties. To transfer:

1. **Manufacturer warranty**: File a transfer request with the manufacturer (fee: $50-150 typically)
2. **Contractor labor warranty**: Confirm with the contractor — some allow transfers, others don't
3. **Extended warranty**: Check the terms — most manufacturer extended warranties are transferable for a fee

Include warranty transfer documentation in your home sale disclosures. A transferable HVAC warranty is a selling point in South Florida's competitive real estate market.
