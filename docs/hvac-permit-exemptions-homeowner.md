# HVAC Permit Exemptions in Florida: What Homeowners and Contractors Need to Know

Florida law requires permits for most HVAC work, but there are limited exemptions that homeowners and contractors should understand before starting a project. Proceeding without a required permit can result in doubled permit fees, stop-work orders, and — in the worst cases — insurance claim denials after a system failure.

## The General Rule

Florida Building Code (FBC) Chapter 1, Section 105 governs when permits are required. The general rule: **any replacement, modification, or new installation of a mechanical system requires a permit from the local building department.**

This includes:
- New AC system installation (replacement or new construction)
- Ductwork modification or replacement
- Condenser relocation
- Refrigerant circuit modifications
- Electrical work associated with HVAC changes

## What IS Exempt (Limited Exceptions)

The exemptions are narrow and often misunderstood. Here's what actually qualifies:

### 1. Like-for-Like Replacement Under 25 Amps
A permit is NOT required for like-for-like replacement of an individual fixture or device — such as a thermostat, contactor, or relay — provided the replacement does not exceed 25 amps and does not modify the existing circuit.

**Example:** Replacing a thermostat with an identical model = OK. Upgrading to a smart thermostat that requires a C-wire (new wiring) = borderline — some jurisdictions require a permit for the new wire.

### 2. Minor Repair (Not Replacement)
The FBC exempts "ordinary repairs" that do not change the structure, occupancy, or fire-safety characteristics of a building. For HVAC, this typically means:

- Cleaning and servicing existing equipment (coil cleaning, filter changes, drain line clearing)
- Replacing an access panel or grille with the same size/type
- Repairing a refrigerant leak on an existing system — wait, this actually DOES require a permit in most jurisdictions because it involves opening the sealed system. See our [permit requirements guide](permits.md) for the full breakdown.

### 3. Agricultural Buildings
HVAC work in agricultural buildings used exclusively for farming purposes is generally exempt from Florida building permits, though federal refrigerant handling rules (EPA Section 608) still apply.

### 4. Emergency Repairs (Conditional)
Some jurisdictions allow emergency HVAC repairs without a prior permit, provided:
- The repair is necessary to protect health or safety (e.g., complete AC failure in a nursing home, or no cooling in a residence during a heat advisory)
- The contractor applies for a permit within the next business day
- The work is inspected retroactively

This is NOT blanket permission to skip permits — it's a grace period for genuine emergencies. A [licensed Florida HVAC contractor](https://ac-repair.today/) should handle the permit filing.

## Common Misconceptions

### "It's the same size unit, so no permit needed"
**False.** Replacing an AC with an identical-capacity unit still requires a permit in every Florida jurisdiction we're aware of. The building department needs to verify:
- Electrical disconnect meets current code
- Refrigerant line connections are properly brazed
- Condenser placement meets current setback and clearance requirements
- System is properly charged and commissioned

### "No structural changes = no permit"
**False.** HVAC work involves mechanical, electrical, and refrigerant systems that are regulated independently of structural changes. A failed HVAC installation can cause mold, fire, and carbon monoxide issues — all of which the permit process is designed to prevent.

### "The contractor said I don't need one"
**Verify it yourself.** Some unlicensed contractors recommend skipping permits because they aren't registered with the local building department. Florida Statute 489.127 makes it a third-degree felony to perform contracting work without a license. Always verify a contractor's license through the DBPR website before hiring.

## Risks of Skipping the Permit

### Financial Risks
- **Double permit fees** — Most jurisdictions charge 2× the normal fee when a permit is applied for after work begins (no-inspection penalty)
- **Insurance denial** — If an unpermitted HVAC installation causes a fire, flood, or structural issue, your homeowner's insurance may deny the claim
- **Selling the home** — Florida real estate disclosure forms require sellers to disclose known unpermitted work. Buyers' attorneys increasingly request permit records for recent HVAC replacements

### Legal Risks
- **Code enforcement violations** — Fines that escalate daily until the violation is corrected
- **Stop-work orders** — Can delay your project indefinitely
- **Liability exposure** — If an unpermitted installation injures someone, the property owner shares liability

### Safety Risks
- Improperly installed systems can leak carbon monoxide (gas furnaces), create electrical hazards, or fail to provide adequate ventilation
- Unpermitted work is never inspected, so hidden problems go undetected

## How the Permit Process Actually Works

1. Contractor pulls the permit (homeowners in some jurisdictions can pull their own — but this is rare and carries insurance risks)
2. Building department reviews the load calculation (Manual J) and equipment selection (Manual S)
3. Rough-in inspection — lineset, electrical disconnect, condensate drain, ductwork
4. Final inspection — system startup, proper operation, refrigerant charge verification
5. Certificate of completion filed for the property record

Typical HVAC permit fees in South Florida range from $75–250 depending on the county. A professional [AC installation](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-installation/) contractor includes permit fees in their estimate.
