# Florida HVAC License Types

Florida regulates HVAC work through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). All HVAC contractors must hold one of several license categories.

## License Categories

### CAC - Class A Air Conditioning Contractor

The most common HVAC license in Florida. Holders can perform:

- Installation, repair, and maintenance of air conditioning systems up to 25 tons cooling capacity
- Ductwork fabrication and installation
- Refrigerant handling (with EPA 608 certification)
- System design for residential and light commercial
- Thermostat and control system installation

**Requirements:**
- 4 years of verifiable HVAC experience (or 3 years + approved technical program)
- Pass the DBPR exam (business + trade sections)
- $100,000 minimum net worth or bond
- Liability insurance ($300,000 minimum)
- Workers compensation coverage

### CMC - Class B Air Conditioning Contractor (Mechanical)

Unlimited mechanical contractor license. Can perform all work a CAC can, plus:

- Systems over 25 tons
- Large commercial and industrial HVAC
- Chiller systems
- Boiler installation
- Process piping related to HVAC

**Requirements:**
- 5 years of verifiable experience in mechanical contracting
- Pass the CMC exam
- Higher bonding and insurance requirements

### RA - Registered HVAC Contractor

County-level registration (not statewide). Limited to the county where registered.

- Same scope as CAC but only valid in one county
- Cannot advertise or work outside the registered county
- Being phased out in many Florida counties

## Specialty Certifications

| Certification | Required For |
|--------------|-------------|
| EPA 608 Universal | Any refrigerant handling |
| EPA 608 Type II | High-pressure refrigerants (most residential AC) |
| NATE Certification | Not required by Florida, but demonstrates competency |
| OSHA 10/30 | Recommended for jobsite safety compliance |

## How to Verify

Every Florida-licensed contractor has a searchable record:

1. Go to [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp)
2. Search by license number (starts with CAC or CMC) or contractor name
3. Verify: license is active, not expired, no disciplinary actions

**Red flags:**
- License shows "Delinquent" or "Suspended" status
- License number doesn't match the name on the business card
- Contractor refuses to provide license number
- License is an RA type but contractor is advertising across county lines

## Important Notes

- **Handyman exemption does NOT apply to HVAC**: Florida's unlicensed handyman exemption ($2,500 threshold) explicitly excludes HVAC work. Any refrigerant handling, ductwork, or system installation requires a license regardless of project cost.
- **Permit pulling**: Only the licensed contractor (or their qualifying agent) can pull HVAC permits. Homeowners cannot pull their own permits for HVAC work.
- **Subcontracting**: A licensed contractor can subcontract HVAC work, but they remain responsible for code compliance and permit closure.
