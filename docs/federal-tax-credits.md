# Federal Tax Credits and Rebates for HVAC Upgrades (2026)

The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) of 2022 created the most substantial set of residential energy efficiency incentives in U.S. history. Many of these credits apply directly to HVAC equipment and are available through 2032. This guide covers what Florida homeowners can claim, how much it's worth, and what qualifies.

## Two Types of Federal Incentives

The IRA created two distinct programs for residential HVAC upgrades:

### 1. Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C)

**How it works**: A tax credit — a dollar-for-dollar reduction in the federal income tax you owe. Not a deduction, not a rebate. If you owe $5,000 in taxes and claim a $2,000 credit, you pay $3,000.

**Annual limit**: $3,200 per year (combined across all qualified improvements). The limit resets each tax year through 2032, so you can spread upgrades across years.

**Sub-limits by category**:
- **$2,000** for heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, and biomass stoves
- **$1,200** for all other qualifying improvements (insulation, windows, doors, central AC, furnaces, electrical panel upgrades)

### 2. High-Efficiency Electric Home Rebate Act (HEEHRA)

**How it works**: A point-of-sale rebate — the discount is applied at the time of purchase, reducing what you pay upfront. Unlike 25C, this is income-dependent.

**Income eligibility**:
- **Under 80% of Area Median Income (AMI)**: Up to 100% of costs covered, subject to per-item caps
- **80-150% of AMI**: Up to 50% of costs covered, subject to per-item caps
- **Over 150% of AMI**: Not eligible for HEEHRA (but still eligible for 25C tax credits)

**Florida AMI note**: AMI varies by county. Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach each have different thresholds. Check your county's current AMI at HUD's income limits page.

## What HVAC Equipment Qualifies

### Heat Pumps — Up to $2,000 (25C) or $8,000 (HEEHRA)

Heat pumps are the highest-priority equipment under both programs.

**25C credit**: 30% of equipment and installation costs, up to $2,000 per year.

**HEEHRA rebate**: Up to $8,000 for qualifying heat pump systems (income-dependent).

**Qualifying criteria**:
- Must meet or exceed ENERGY STAR requirements
- For 25C: CEE Tier 1 or higher efficiency rating
- Split systems: minimum 15.2 SEER2 and 7.8 HSPF2 (higher than Florida minimum code)
- Must be installed by a licensed contractor

**Why this matters in Florida**: Despite the name, heat pumps are air conditioners that can also heat. In South Florida's mild winters, a heat pump replaces both your AC and any supplemental heating with a single, more efficient system. The federal incentives make the price difference between a standard AC and a heat pump negligible or even negative.

### Central Air Conditioners — Up to $600 (25C)

**25C credit**: 30% of equipment and installation costs, up to $600.

**Qualifying criteria**:
- ENERGY STAR Most Efficient certification
- Split systems: minimum 16 SEER2
- Package systems: minimum 15.2 SEER2

**Note**: Central AC does NOT qualify for HEEHRA rebates. If you're choosing between a standard AC and a heat pump, the rebate math strongly favors the heat pump.

### Furnaces and Boilers — Up to $600 (25C)

Less relevant in South Florida where heating is minimal, but included for completeness:
- Gas furnaces: 97% AFUE minimum
- Oil furnaces: 95% AFUE minimum
- Boilers: 95% AFUE minimum (gas) or 90% AFUE minimum (oil)

### Ductwork and Air Sealing — Up to $1,200 (25C) or $1,600 (HEEHRA)

**25C credit**: Insulation, air sealing, and duct sealing qualify under the $1,200 envelope category at 30%.

**HEEHRA rebate**: Up to $1,600 for insulation, air sealing, and ventilation (income-dependent).

This includes:
- Duct sealing (Aeroseal or manual)
- Attic insulation upgrades
- Air sealing of the building envelope
- Ventilation improvements (ERV/HRV installation)

### Electrical Panel Upgrades — Up to $600 (25C) or $4,000 (HEEHRA)

If your HVAC upgrade requires a panel upgrade (common when switching from gas to electric heat pump), the panel work may also qualify:

- **25C**: 30% up to $600 for a panel upgrade to 200 amps or higher
- **HEEHRA**: Up to $4,000 for electrical service upgrades (income-dependent)

### Thermostat — Does NOT Qualify

Smart thermostats were removed from the 25C credit starting in 2023. They may still qualify for utility rebates (see below).

## How to Claim the 25C Credit

1. **Purchase qualifying equipment** and have it installed by a licensed contractor
2. **Keep all documentation**: manufacturer certification statement (most manufacturers provide this), itemized invoice showing equipment cost and labor, and proof of payment
3. **File IRS Form 5695** with your annual tax return
4. **Apply the credit** on your Form 1040

The credit is non-refundable — it reduces your tax liability to zero but won't generate a refund. If your tax bill is less than your credit, you lose the excess (it does not carry forward).

## How to Claim HEEHRA Rebates

HEEHRA is administered at the state level. Florida's program is managed through the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS). As of 2026:

1. **Check eligibility** at the program portal (income verification required)
2. **Get pre-approval** before purchasing equipment
3. **Use a participating contractor** — the rebate is applied at point of sale
4. **Submit documentation** through the state portal

Processing times vary. Some states launched their HEEHRA programs in 2024-2025; check the current status of Florida's program before planning around it.

## Florida Utility Rebates (Stackable)

Federal credits and rebates can be combined with utility company rebates. Major Florida utilities offer:

### FPL (Florida Power & Light)

- High-efficiency AC rebate: varies by SEER2 rating
- Duct testing and sealing rebate
- Smart thermostat rebate
- Building envelope improvements
- Check current programs at FPL's website

### Duke Energy Florida

- Central AC and heat pump rebates
- Duct repair rebates
- Attic insulation rebates
- Check current programs at Duke Energy's website

### TECO (Tampa Electric)

- Similar programs to FPL and Duke
- Specific to the Tampa Bay service area

**Stacking order**: Utility rebates reduce your out-of-pocket cost. The federal 25C credit is calculated on your remaining cost after rebates. This means you can potentially cover 50-70% of a qualifying HVAC upgrade through combined incentives.

## Example: Heat Pump Upgrade in Miami-Dade

| Line Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| 16 SEER2 heat pump (equipment + install) | $9,500 |
| FPL rebate | -$400 |
| Net cost | $9,100 |
| 25C credit (30% of $9,100, capped at $2,000) | -$2,000 |
| Effective cost | $7,100 |

If the homeowner qualifies for HEEHRA (under 80% AMI), the rebate could cover up to $8,000 — potentially making the upgrade nearly free.

## Important Rules and Gotchas

1. **New installations only** — you cannot claim credits for equipment installed in a new construction home. The home must be an existing residence.
2. **Primary residence** — 25C applies only to your primary home, not rental properties or second homes.
3. **Licensed contractor required** — DIY installations do not qualify.
4. **Annual reset** — the $3,200 annual limit resets each January 1. Plan large upgrades across two calendar years to maximize credits.
5. **25C and HEEHRA stack** — you can use HEEHRA for the upfront rebate AND claim 25C on remaining costs (but cannot double-count the same expense).
6. **Keep records for 3 years** — the IRS can audit your return for up to 3 years after filing.

## Timeline

These incentives are available through December 31, 2032. Equipment must be purchased AND installed within the tax year you're claiming. There is no guarantee these programs will be extended beyond 2032.
