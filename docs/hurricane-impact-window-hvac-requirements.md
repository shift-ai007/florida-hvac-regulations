# Hurricane Impact Windows and HVAC: Ventilation, Combustion Air, and Code Requirements

South Florida homeowners installing hurricane impact windows and doors often discover an unintended consequence: the home's new airtight envelope may conflict with HVAC and combustion appliance code requirements. This guide covers the interplay between impact-rated openings and mechanical system ventilation requirements under the Florida Building Code (FBC).

## The Airtight Home Problem

Impact windows and doors are designed to prevent air infiltration during hurricane-force winds. When installed in an older home that previously relied on leaky windows for passive ventilation, the result can be:

- **Insufficient combustion air** for gas or propane appliances (water heaters, furnaces, boilers, pool heaters, dryers)
- **Negative pressure** that prevents exhaust fans (bathroom, kitchen, clothes dryer) from venting properly
- **Elevated indoor humidity** because natural air exchange drops from 0.5–1.0 ACH (air changes per hour) to 0.1–0.2 ACH
- **Radon or soil gas accumulation** in homes with slab foundations

## Combustion Air Requirements (FBC Mechanical Chapter 7)

### Indoor Combustion Air (Standard Method)

If the mechanical room draws combustion air from inside the home (the older, pre-impact-window approach), FBC M701 requires:

- Two permanent openings to the interior: one within 12 inches of the ceiling, one within 12 inches of the floor
- Each opening must be sized at 1 square inch per 1,000 BTU/h of total appliance input
- Combined appliance input in the space must not exceed that volume × 100 BTU/h per cubic foot

**The problem**: Impact windows dramatically reduce the infiltration rate that previously replenished the combustion air drawn from inside. A home that passed combustion air calculations in the 1990s may fail today because the actual infiltration rate is now an order of magnitude lower.

### Direct Vent (Sealed Combustion)

The safest solution when installing impact windows in a home with gas appliances: replace atmospherically vented water heaters and furnaces with **direct-vent (sealed combustion)** units. These draw combustion air directly from outdoors through a dedicated PVC pipe, completely independent of indoor air. This eliminates the combustion air calculation entirely.

**Florida code**: FBC M701.2 permits direct-vent appliances without additional combustion air openings. This is the preferred solution for energy-efficient homes.

### Outdoor Combustion Air (Ducted)

If replacing all appliances is not practical, a dedicated combustion air duct from outdoors to the mechanical room is the alternative. Requirements:

- Duct sized at 1 square inch per 4,000 BTU/h (horizontal) or 1 per 3,000 BTU/h (vertical)
- Duct must terminate in a screened intake hood at least 12 inches above grade and 24 inches from any impact window opening
- The intake hood must be impact-rated or protected by an impact-rated exterior enclosure
- A motorized damper interlocked with appliance operation prevents air leakage when appliances are off

## HRV/ERV Requirements for Airtight Homes

When a home's natural infiltration drops below 0.35 ACH (the ASHRAE 62.2 ventilation standard), mechanical ventilation is required. FBC Chapter 4 references ASHRAE 62.2-2019 for residential ventilation:

| Bedrooms | Floor Area (sq ft) | Required CFM Continuous |
|----------|-------------------|------------------------|
| 2 | ≤ 1,500 | 45 CFM |
| 3 | 1,501–2,000 | 60 CFM |
| 4 | 2,001–2,500 | 75 CFM |
| 5 | 2,501–3,000 | 90 CFM |

**Options for meeting this requirement**:

- **HRV/ERV system**: A heat recovery ventilator (HRV) or energy recovery ventilator (ERV) provides continuous mechanical ventilation while recovering 60–85% of the energy from exhaust air. ERVs are preferred in South Florida because they transfer some moisture load, reducing the dehumidification burden on the AC system.
- **Bathroom exhaust fan**: A continuously running (or occupancy-sensed) bathroom exhaust fan meeting the CFM requirement, with a backdraft damper and exterior impact-rated hood.
- **Central fan integrated ventilation**: The HVAC system blower runs on a scheduled cycle (typically 20–30 minutes per hour) with a motorized fresh air damper. Requires that the thermostat supports this mode — many smart thermostats do.

## Impact on AC Sizing and Performance

A home that had its windows replaced with impact-rated units after the AC was installed may now find the original system is oversized. The reduced heat gain from improved air sealing means:

- Shorter cooling cycles (less sensible load)
- Poor dehumidification (the AC satisfies the thermostat faster but runs less total time, removing less moisture)
- Higher indoor humidity despite lower energy bills

**The fix**: A [professional AC maintenance](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-maintenance/) visit should include a Manual J load calculation update after window replacement. If the AC is now oversized by more than 30%, a variable-speed system or whole-house dehumidifier may be needed.

## South Florida County Variations

| County | Additional Requirements |
|--------|----------------------|
| Miami-Dade | NOA (Notice of Acceptance) required for all impact-rated intake/exhaust hoods. Must meet TAS 201 impact protocol. |
| Broward | Broward County Amendment 35-11 requires HRV/ERV systems in any substantial renovation (>50% window replacement) |
| Palm Beach | Follows FBC base code. Strong recommendation for combustion safety testing after impact window installation (not a code requirement but a NFPA 54 recommendation) |

## Carbon Monoxide Detector Requirements

After impact window installation, Florida building code (FBC R315) and NFPA 720 require:

- CO detectors in any home with fuel-burning appliances
- Detectors placed within 15 feet of each sleeping room
- Detectors on each habitable level
- Hardwired with battery backup (or sealed 10-year lithium battery type)

This requirement applies retroactively when the homeowner applies for the window permit — the building department will typically require verification of CO detector compliance before finaling the window permit.
