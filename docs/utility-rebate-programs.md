# Florida Utility Rebate Programs for HVAC

Upgrading to a high-efficiency HVAC system in Florida comes with significant savings from multiple rebate sources. Federal tax credits, state utility rebates, and manufacturer incentives can stack to cover 30–50% of equipment costs.

This guide covers Florida-specific utility rebate programs from the state's major electric companies, how to combine them with federal credits, and what South Florida homeowners need to know before they buy.

## How HVAC Rebates Stack in Florida

The maximum savings come from stacking three layers:

```
Layer 1: Federal tax credit (25C) — up to $2,000
Layer 2: Utility rebate — $150–$1,500 depending on utility
Layer 3: Manufacturer rebate — $200–$1,000 (seasonal promotions)
Total potential: $500–$4,500+ per system
```

**Important**: Federal tax credits are claimed on your tax return (not instant). Utility and manufacturer rebates are typically applied as a discount or mailed as a check. Manufacturer rebates often require registration for the specific promotion.

## Federal Tax Credits (25C — Extended Through 2032)

The Inflation Reduction Act extended and expanded the **Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit** (25C) through 2032:

| Equipment | Minimum Efficiency | Credit Amount | Cap |
|-----------|------------------|---------------|-----|
| Central AC (split system) | 16 SEER2 / 12 EER2 | 30% of cost | $2,000 |
| Central AC (single package) | 16 SEER2 / 13 EER2 | 30% of cost | $2,000 |
| Air source heat pump | 15.2 SEER2 / 8.1 HSPF2 | 30% of cost | $2,000 |
| Natural gas furnace | 95% AFUE | 30% of cost | $2,000 |
| Heat pump water heater | UEF ≥ 3.3 | 30% of cost | $2,000 |

**Rules**:
- Annual cap of $2,000 total for all HVAC credits combined
- Equipment must meet or exceed ENERGY STAR most-efficient requirements
- Installation must be in an existing home (your primary residence) — new construction does not qualify
- Must be installed by a licensed contractor (not DIY)
- Cap is _per year_ — if you replace both AC and furnace in the same year, you're capped at $2,000 combined, not per appliance

For more on the federal side, see our [Federal Tax Credits & Rebates guide](federal-tax-credits.md).

## Florida Power & Light (FPL) Rebates

FPL serves most of South Florida including Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties — the largest utility in the state.

### FPL Duct Repair/Replacement Program

| Detail | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Rebate amount | $300–$900 (up to 50% of cost) |
| Requirement | Existing ducts must leak >15% (confirmed by diagnostic test) |
| Eligibility | FPL residential customers with ducted systems |
| How to apply | FPL-approved contractor performs test-in, repair, test-out |
| Frequency | Once per property |

### FPL High-Efficiency AC Rebate

**Note**: FPL's direct AC rebate program has been discontinued as of 2025. FPL has shifted toward demand response programs (On Call/Connected Savings) and residential energy efficiency audits instead. Homeowners should check FPL's current incentive page for updated offerings — programs change year to year.

### FPL On Call (Connected Savings)

FPL's demand response program for central AC:

- **Incentive**: $25–$50 annual credit on your bill (plus a $75–$100 signup bonus in some years)
- **How it works**: FPL installs a smart thermostat or control switch on your AC. During peak demand events (typically 5–10 summer afternoons per year), FPL cycles your AC for 15–30 minutes
- **Opt-out option**: You can override any event
- **Requirement**: Central AC system in good working order
- **Stackability**: Can be combined with any manufacturer rebate or tax credit

## Manufacturer Rebates

HVAC manufacturers offer seasonal promotions that vary by region and time of year. Common South Florida patterns:

### Spring/Summer (March–July)
- **Carrier/Bryant**: Up to $1,000 rebate on qualifying heat pump or AC + furnace combination
- **Trane/American Standard**: $200–$800 rebate on 17+ SEER systems
- **Lennox**: Up to $1,000 rebate on high-end systems with variable-speed equipment
- **Daikin/Amana/Goodman**: $200–$600 rebate on 16+ SEER systems

### Fall/Winter (August–December)
- Lower rebate amounts typically ($100–$400)
- Better pricing on furnaces (not helpful in South Florida)
- Sometimes "free extended warranty" promotions instead of cash rebates

### How to Get the Best Deal
- **Timing**: Buy in spring (March–May) when seasonal promos start and installation schedules are open. Summer (June–August) is peak season — contractors are busy, prices are highest, and rebate funds may be exhausted.
- **Online registration**: Most manufacturers require online rebate registration within 30–60 days of installation. Missing this window forfeits the rebate.
- **Contractor**: Only authorized dealers can pass through manufacturer rebates. Verify the contractor carries the brand's "Preferred" or "Factory Authorized" designation.

## Common Mistakes That Cost You Rebates

### Buying Before Checking Eligibility
The #1 mistake: homeowners replace their AC and then look for rebates. Many utility programs require **pre-approval** or contractor enrollment before installation.

**Fix**: Before signing a contract, ask your utility: "Do I need to enroll or get pre-approved before my AC replacement?"

### Equipment Efficiency Just Below the Cutoff
Rebate tiers often have sharp efficiency cutoffs: 16 SEER2 qualifies, 15.8 SEER2 does not. The price difference between a 15.5 and 16 SEER2 system is typically $200–$400 — but you lose a $400–$2,000 rebate.

**Fix**: Ask your contractor for the exact SEER2 rating (not just the model number) and verify it against rebate requirements before buying.

### Missing the Registration Window
Manufacturer rebates require online registration within 30 days of installation. Many contractors fail to mention this. The rebate is forfeited if you miss the deadline.

**Fix**: When your system is installed, immediately register all equipment with the manufacturer and submit rebate forms online. Set a calendar reminder.

### Assuming Tax Credits Are Automatic
Federal tax credits require filing **IRS Form 5695** with your annual return. You need:
- Manufacturer's Certification Statement (from the manufacturer's website — download and save now)
- Installer's name and license number
- Date of installation
- Total cost

**Fix**: Ask your contractor for the Manufacturer's Certification Statement at installation time (not six months later when you're filing taxes). Save it with your tax records.
