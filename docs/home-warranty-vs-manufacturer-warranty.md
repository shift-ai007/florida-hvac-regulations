# Home Warranty vs. Manufacturer Warranty: A Florida Homeowner's Guide

Florida homeowners frequently confuse home warranties with manufacturer warranties — and the difference matters enormously when an AC system fails on a July afternoon in Miami-Dade County. Using the wrong warranty can leave you paying out of pocket for a repair you thought was covered.

This guide explains how each warranty type works, what they cover, and how to navigate claims in South Florida.

## The Core Difference in One Sentence

A **manufacturer warranty** covers defects in materials and workmanship of the equipment itself. A **home warranty** is a service contract that covers repair or replacement of major home systems (including AC) due to normal wear and tear.

They are not interchangeable. They do not overlap. And having one does not eliminate the need for the other.

## Manufacturer Warranties: What They Actually Cover

### Standard Coverage (Included With Every System)

All major AC manufacturers — Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, Daikin, Goodman — provide a standard parts warranty with new equipment. In 2026, these typically include:

| Component | Standard Warranty Period | What It Covers |
|-----------|------------------------|----------------|
| Compressor | 10 years (residential) | Defects — sealed system failure, valve failure, electrical winding failure |
| Evaporator coil | 10 years (residential) | Defects — leaks, manufacturing defects in tubing or fins |
| Condenser coil | 10 years (residential) | Defects — leaks, corrosion from manufacturing flaws |
| Control board | 5-10 years (varies) | Component failure not caused by power surge or misuse |
| Other parts | 5-10 years (varies) | Fan motors, capacitors, contactors, reversing valves |

**What manufacturer warranties do NOT cover:**
- Labor costs (typically $300-800 per repair visit)
- Refrigerant (unless the leak is from a warrantied coil defect — and even then, some manufacturers limit refrigerant coverage)
- Damage from improper installation, poor maintenance, or power surges
- Outdoor coil corrosion from salt air (see note below)
- Maintenance visits, cleaning, or tune-ups

### The Registration Trap

Most manufacturer warranties are automatically 5 years from the date of installation **unless the homeowner registers the product online within 60-90 days of installation**. Registration upgrades the warranty to 10 years.

This is one of the most common warranty failures in Florida. A homeowner buys a new AC system, the contractor installs it, and somewhere in the paperwork shuffle the warranty is never registered. Three years later, when the evaporator coil leaks, the homeowner discovers the parts warranty is only 5 years — and they are year 4.

**Always register the warranty yourself within a week of installation.** The contractor may offer to do it, but if they forget or the paperwork sits on a desk, you lose coverage. The registration requires:
- Model and serial numbers (on the outdoor unit nameplate)
- Date of installation
- Contractor name and license number
- Your contact information

### The Corrosion Exclusion

Many manufacturers explicitly exclude corrosion damage from "corrosive environment" — which includes coastal salt air. For South Florida homes within 3 miles of the coast, this exclusion means that a coil that fails from formicary corrosion (a common failure mode in our climate) may not be covered even within the warranty period.

Some manufacturers offer upgraded "coastal" or "marine" warranty options that specifically cover corrosion. If you are within sight of salt water, ask your contractor about purchasing the upgraded corrosion warranty at the time of installation. It costs $150-300 and adds 5-10 years of corrosion coverage.

### Filing a Manufacturer Warranty Claim

When an AC component fails:
1. **Contact the installing contractor** first — they will diagnose, verify the failure, and contact the manufacturer's distributor
2. **The contractor files a warranty claim** with the manufacturer through their distributor
3. **The manufacturer approves or denies** based on the diagnosed failure mode
4. **If approved**: the manufacturer ships a replacement part to the distributor (usually 1-5 days)
5. **You pay labor** to the contractor for the repair visit and installation

The process takes 2-7 days in most cases. If the system is completely non-functional and the replacement part is backordered (which happens during Florida's summer peak when demand surges), a good contractor will identify a compatible substitute or expedite through their network. ## Home Warranties: A Different Animal

### How Home Warranties Work

A home warranty is a year-long service contract you purchase separately from your homeowners insurance or as a standalone policy. When an AC system fails, you:

1. **Contact the home warranty company** — they have a call center, not a technician
2. **Pay a service fee** (typically $75-150 per visit)
3. **A contracted technician is dispatched** — you do not choose the company
4. **The technician diagnoses and reports** back to the warranty company
5. **The warranty company decides** whether to approve or deny coverage
6. **If approved**: the contractor repairs within the warranty's coverage limits
7. **You pay the service fee + any uncovered costs**

### Coverage Limits That Matter

Home warranty AC coverage is not "everything is fixed for free." Typical coverage caps:

| Item | Typical Home Warranty | Why It Matters |
|------|----------------------|----------------|
| Per-repair coverage cap | $1,500-2,500 | A compressor replacement costs $2,500-3,500; you pay the difference |
| Annual coverage cap | $3,000-5,000 | Multiple failures in one year exhaust coverage quickly |
| Refrigerant coverage | Included (first pound often) | Large leak requiring 4+ lbs of R-410A may exceed per-incident cap |
| Ductwork | Usually excluded | Most duct repairs are $500-2,000 out of pocket |
| Permit fees | Excluded | Required by Florida law for replacements |
| Code upgrades | Excluded | Required by Florida Building Code for replacements |
| Pre-existing conditions | Excluded | If the technician notes evidence of an old problem, coverage is denied |

### The "Maintenance Record" Gotcha

Home warranty companies require proof of annual professional maintenance to honor AC claims. When you file a claim, the warranty company asks for maintenance records. Without them, they can deny coverage on the basis that "lack of maintenance caused or contributed to the failure."

Florida homeowners who buy a home warranty when their system is new, then skip professional maintenance for three years, discover this clause only when their claim is denied. Keep your maintenance invoices — they are your proof of coverage. You can verify maintenance requirements with your contractor during an [AC maintenance](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-maintenance/) visit.

### The South Florida Replacement Problem

If your AC system fails completely and needs replacement, a home warranty typically:
- Pays a fixed amount toward the replacement (often $1,000-1,500)
- Covers a "builder-grade" basic replacement system
- Does not cover code-required upgrades (SEER2 minimum, A2L compliance, Miami-Dade NOA)
- Does not cover ductwork modifications or plenum repairs

In practice, a homeowner using a home warranty for AC replacement in South Florida often pays $4,000-8,000 out of pocket for the difference between the warranty allowance and a proper licensed install with permits. The warranty helps, but it is not "free AC replacement." For a smooth process with minimal out-of-pocket gap, work with a contractor experienced in navigating warranty + home warranty coordination.

## Which Warranty Applies in Common Scenarios

| Scenario | Use Manufacturer Warranty | Use Home Warranty | Pay Out of Pocket |
|----------|--------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 2-year-old system — compressor locked up | ✓ Parts covered (10yr), labor not covered ($400-800) | Maybe — check if replacement cap covers labor | Labor if home warranty declines |
| 8-year-old system — capacitor failed | Expired — 5yr basic was not registered | ✓ Service fee only ($75-150) | — |
| 12-year-old system — indoor coil leak | Expired | ✓ Up to per-repair cap | Costs above cap |
| 15-year-old system — compressor failed | Expired | $1,500-2,500 toward replacement | Remaining replacement cost ($4k-8k) |
| Evaporator coil leak at year 4 | ✓ Parts (if registered 10yr) | Not needed if manufacturer covers parts | Labor only |
| Outdoor coil corrosion at year 5 | Likely denied (corrosion exclusion) | Maybe — depends on wear and tear vs corrosion clause | Possibly all |

## Practical Recommendations

### For New System Purchases
1. Register the manufacturer warranty yourself within 7 days of installation
2. Consider the extended labor warranty (adds 5-10 years of labor coverage — typically $400-600)
3. If within 3 miles of the coast, buy the corrosion upgraded warranty
4. Keep the installation contract, model/serial numbers, and registration confirmation in a file

### For Existing Systems
1. Dig up your warranty registration document or check online with the manufacturer
2. Know your system's age and components (find the data plate on the outdoor unit)
3. Decide whether a home warranty makes sense — for systems 8-15 years old in Florida's climate, the per-incident cap of $1,500-2,500 often limits coverage to minor repairs
4. Maintain the system annually and keep the invoices as proof for potential home warranty claims

### When Filing a Claim
1. Read both warranty documents before calling a technician — know which warranty applies
2. With manufacturer warranty: call an independent HVAC contractor (not the warranty company's network)
3. With home warranty: document everything. Take photos of the failed equipment, the model/serial plate, and the technician's written diagnosis
4. Be prepared to push back on denials — warranty claim denial is common but not always correct
