# HVAC Insurance, Warranties, and Liability in Florida

Understanding the insurance and warranty landscape for HVAC work in Florida protects both homeowners and contractors. This guide covers what's required, what protects you, and what voids your coverage.

## Contractor Insurance Requirements

Florida law requires licensed HVAC contractors to carry specific insurance before they can pull permits or do work legally.

### Required Coverage

| Insurance Type | Minimum Coverage | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| General Liability | $300,000 per occurrence | Covers property damage, injuries at the job site |
| Workers' Compensation | Required if 1+ employees | Covers worker injuries — without it, the HOMEOWNER can be liable |
| Auto Liability | State minimums | Covers company vehicles at your property |

### Why Workers' Comp Matters to Homeowners

If a contractor without workers' comp sends an employee to your home and that employee is injured, **you could be held liable** under Florida law. The employee can file a claim against your homeowner's insurance.

**Always ask**: "Do you carry workers' compensation insurance?" If they say they're exempt (sole proprietors with no employees can be exempt), verify it at the Florida Division of Workers' Compensation: [myfloridacfo.com](https://www.myfloridacfo.com).

## Homeowner's Insurance and HVAC

### What's Typically Covered

- **Sudden AC failure causing water damage** — If your air handler's drain pan overflows and damages the ceiling below, the water damage repair is typically covered (minus deductible). The AC repair itself is not
- **Storm damage to the outdoor unit** — Condenser damaged by a falling tree or flying debris during a hurricane is typically covered under dwelling protection
- **Electrical surge damage** — A lightning strike that fries your AC control board may be covered

### What's Typically NOT Covered

- **Normal wear and tear** — A compressor that fails at 15 years old is not a covered event
- **Maintenance-related failures** — If a clogged drain line causes water damage and you can't show regular maintenance, the claim may be denied
- **Improper installation** — If an AC was installed without permits and it causes damage, insurance may deny the claim and pursue the contractor
- **Gradual leaks** — A slow refrigerant leak that causes the compressor to overheat and fail — this is maintenance, not a sudden event
- **Code compliance upgrades** — If your AC needs to be replaced and current code requires a more expensive system than what you had, the code-upgrade cost is often not covered unless you have "Ordinance or Law" coverage

### Protecting Your Coverage

1. **Keep maintenance records** — Annual professional tune-up receipts. If you ever file a water damage or mold claim, the adjuster will ask about maintenance history
2. **Pull permits for installations** — Unpermitted HVAC work can void coverage for related claims
3. **Use licensed contractors** — Work done by unlicensed individuals may not be covered if it causes damage
4. **Document your system** — Keep model numbers, serial numbers, installation dates, and warranty information in one place

## Manufacturer Warranties

### Standard Coverage

Most major HVAC manufacturers (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman) offer:

| Component | Typical Warranty | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Compressor | 10 years (some offer limited lifetime) | Must register within 60–90 days of installation |
| Parts (coils, motors, controls) | 5–10 years | Registration required |
| Labor | 1 year standard (extended available) | Varies by manufacturer and installer |

### What Voids a Manufacturer Warranty

- **Not registering the system** — Most manufacturers require online registration within 60–90 days. Without it, the warranty drops to 5 years on parts
- **Unlicensed installation** — If the system wasn't installed by a licensed contractor, the warranty is void
- **Improper installation** — Failure to follow manufacturer specs (wrong line set size, improper electrical, no condensate trap). The manufacturer's technician will inspect before approving a claim
- **No maintenance records** — Some manufacturers require proof of annual professional maintenance to honor warranty claims
- **Unauthorized modifications** — Adding aftermarket parts, modifying the refrigerant charge without documentation, or installing non-approved accessories

### Extended Warranties

Most installing contractors offer extended labor warranties (2–5 years) for an additional fee ($200–$800). These cover the labor cost of warranty parts replacement, which the manufacturer warranty does not.

**Worth it?** In South Florida, where systems run hard and failure rates are higher than the national average, a 5-year labor warranty is generally worthwhile — especially for the first few years when hidden installation defects may surface.

### Home Warranty Plans vs Manufacturer Warranties

Home warranty plans (American Home Shield, First American, etc.) are **not the same** as manufacturer warranties:

| Feature | Manufacturer Warranty | Home Warranty Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Included with purchase | $400–$800/year + service fees |
| Covers | Defects in materials/workmanship | Breakdown from normal wear |
| Duration | 5–10 years (registered) | Renewed annually |
| Parts quality | OEM parts | Often generic/refurbished |
| Contractor choice | You choose | They assign one |
| Claim approval | Based on defect evidence | Subjective — many denials |

Home warranty plans can make sense for systems out of manufacturer warranty (10+ years old), but read the coverage limitations carefully.

## Florida Construction Lien Law

If your HVAC contractor doesn't pay their suppliers or subcontractors, those unpaid parties can place a lien on **your property** — even if you already paid the contractor in full.

### How to Protect Yourself

1. **Request a Final Waiver and Release of Lien** from the contractor upon final payment
2. **Pay with check or credit card** (not cash) for a paper trail
3. **Verify the contractor's payment history** — a contractor who frequently has liens filed against them is a risk
4. **For large jobs ($2,500+)**, consider using a construction escrow or paying in stages tied to completed milestones

### Lien Timeline

- **Notice to Owner**: Subcontractors/suppliers must send this within 45 days of first furnishing materials/labor. If you receive one, it's a warning — it means someone in the chain wants to make sure they get paid
- **Claim of Lien**: Must be filed within 90 days of completion. This goes on your property record
- **Enforcement**: Lien holder must file a lawsuit within 1 year or the lien expires

## Key Takeaways

1. Always verify contractor insurance (liability + workers' comp) before allowing work
2. Register new HVAC equipment with the manufacturer immediately after installation
3. Keep all maintenance receipts — they protect both warranty claims and insurance claims
4. Pull permits for all HVAC installation work
5. Request lien waivers for any job over $2,500
