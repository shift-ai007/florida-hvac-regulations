# Florida Mold Remediation Licensing for HVAC Contractors

Mold in HVAC systems is a recurring issue in South Florida — our combination of humidity, condensation, and warm temperatures creates ideal conditions for mold growth in ductwork, air handlers, and evaporator coils. Many HVAC contractors offer mold assessment and remediation services, but Florida has specific licensing requirements that differ from general HVAC license scope.

This guide covers what Florida law allows, what requires additional licensing, and how homeowners can verify a contractor's qualifications for mold-related HVAC work.

## The Regulatory Landscape

Florida regulates mold remediation under **Florida Statute 468.84–468.8424** and **Florida Administrative Code 61-31**. Key distinction:

| Activity | License Required | Who Can Do It |
|----------|-----------------|---------------|
| Diagnosing mold in AC | No specific mold license needed | Any licensed HVAC contractor or general contractor |
| Cleaning mold from AC coils/pan | No specific mold license needed | Licensed HVAC contractor or building maintenance |
| Duct cleaning (mold in ducts) | No specific license (but must follow NADCA standards) | Licensed HVAC contractor with duct-cleaning equipment |
| **Mold remediation in ductwork** (removing contaminated material) | **MRSR license required** | Florida Mold Remediation Service Provider (licensed) |
| **Mold assessment** (testing, sampling, clearance) | **MRSA license required** | Florida Mold Remediation Service Assessor (licensed) |
| HVAC repair on system with known mold | No additional license | Licensed HVAC contractor (scope of their CAC/CMC license) |

**Bottom line**: An HVAC contractor can clean a moldy AC coil (that's standard maintenance) and replace mold-damaged duct sections (that's HVAC work). But they cannot perform whole-duct mold remediation, post-remediation clearance testing, or hold themselves out as a "mold remediation company" without the MRSR or MRSA license.

## MRSR License (Mold Remediation Service Provider)

The MRSR license allows a business to perform mold remediation — the physical removal, cleaning, and disposal of mold-contaminated materials.

### Requirements

- **State exam**: Pass the Florida Mold Remediation Service Provider exam
- **Background check**: State and national criminal history
- **Insurance**: General liability ($100,000 minimum) — most HVAC contractors already carry this
- **Registration**: Renew every 2 years with continuing education (6 hours in mold-related coursework per cycle)
- **Supervision**: An MRSR must be the direct supervisor of all remediation work conducted by the business

### Cost

- Application fee: ~$200
- Exam fee: ~$50
- License fee: ~$200 (biennial)
- Prep course: $200–$500

### How HVAC Contractors Usually Obtain It

Most HVAC contractors take a 1–2 day exam prep course offered through FADCO, ACCA Florida, or local trade schools. The exam is not difficult for someone experienced with HVAC systems and building science — the challenging part is the regulatory framework (containment, negative air, waste disposal documentation).

## MRSA License (Mold Remediation Service Assessor)

The MRSA license is for someone who performs mold testing, assessment, and clearance. This is a separate license from MRSR, and **one person cannot hold both for the same job** — the assessor must be independent of the remediator to avoid conflict of interest.

### Why This Matters for HVAC Work

If an HVAC contractor tells you "we'll test your ducts for mold and remediate what we find," they need BOTH an MRSR (for the remediation) and a separate MRSA (for the testing), or they need to subcontract one of the two. A CAC license alone covers cleaning the AC equipment but not duct remediation.

**Practical reality**: Many small HVAC contractors in South Florida do not hold MRSR/MRSA licenses. They subcontract mold testing to a licensed assessor and the remediation portion to a licensed remediator — or they refer the customer to a specialist and handle only the HVAC repair side.

## HVAC-Specific Mold Issues That Don't Require MRSR

The following activities are considered HVAC service work and are within the scope of a valid Florida HVAC license (CAC, CMC, or RA):

### Evaporator Coil Mold
Mold on the evaporator coil surface is caused by dust buildup + condensate moisture. Cleaning the coil is standard HVAC maintenance. The contractor can:
- Chemically clean the coil (with EPA-registered coil cleaner, not biocide)
- Flush the drain pan and line
- Apply a no-rinse coil protectant (some inhibit mold regrowth)

### Condensate Pan Mold
The condensate drain pan is part of the AC equipment. Cleaning it and treating it with a pan tablet is HVAC scope.

### Supply Register Mold
Mold visible at the supply register: the HVAC contractor can clean the register and the first 2–3 feet of accessible duct. Anything beyond that enters duct remediation territory.

### Air Handler Interior
The interior surfaces of the air handler cabinet can be cleaned by the HVAC contractor. The blower wheel and motor can be removed, cleaned, and reinstalled as part of maintenance.

### Duct Repair
If a section of duct is mold-damaged beyond cleaning, the HVAC contractor can:
- Remove and replace the damaged duct section (HVAC scope)
- Seal and insulate the new section
- Identify the moisture source causing the mold

## When a Full MRSR is Required

Call a licensed mold remediation service provider if:

- Mold covers more than 10 square feet in the duct system
- Mold is growing on non-HVAC surfaces (drywall, framing, insulation)
- Occupants have health symptoms attributed to mold exposure
- There's visible mold on both sides of the drywall (behind walls)
- The property involves insurance claims for mold damage
- Post-remediation clearance testing is needed

## Questions for Your HVAC Contractor

When discussing HVAC mold issues:

1. **"Do you hold a Florida MRSR or MRSA license?"** — If they say yes, verify at [DBPR online](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). If they say no, ask who handles that part.

2. **"Who will do the mold assessment?"** — The person testing should be an MRSA, separate from the person doing the cleaning/remediation.

3. **"Will you provide a scope of work?"** — A professional mold remediation plan includes: containment setup, negative air pressure, HEPA vacuuming, cleaning protocol, and post-remediation verification.

4. **"What about the moisture source?"** — Mold remediation without fixing the moisture problem is pointless. A good contractor addresses the leak, humidity, or condensation issue alongside the mold removal.

## South Florida-Specific Considerations

- **Insurance verification**: Many South Florida homeowners policies have mold exclusions (typically $10,000–$15,000 limit for mold damage). Check coverage before authorizing mold remediation. HVAC-related mold (from condensate leaks) is sometimes covered as "water damage" rather than "mold damage" — this distinction matters for billing.

- **Duct cleaning associations**: Legitimate duct-cleaning contractors in South Florida are typically NADCA (National Air Duct Cleaners Association) certified. Ask if the company holds NADCA certification and follows their ACR 2025 standard.

- **Mold-resistant products**: When replacing ducts in mold-prone South Florida homes, specify:
  - Mylar-lined flex duct (smooth interior, less surface for mold)
  - Closed-cell foam insulation (doesn't absorb moisture like fiberglass)
  - Antimicrobial-coated duct board for main supply trunks
