# Florida Homeowner Rights & Protections for HVAC Work

Florida law provides several protections for homeowners when hiring HVAC contractors. Understanding these rights before signing a contract can prevent disputes and protect your investment.

## Construction Lien Law (Florida Statute 713)

### What It Means

Any contractor, subcontractor, or material supplier who performs work on your property can file a lien if they aren't paid. This applies to HVAC work.

### Your Protections

- **Notice to Owner (NTO)**: Subcontractors and suppliers must serve a Notice to Owner within 45 days of starting work. If they don't, they lose lien rights.
- **Contractor's Final Payment Affidavit**: Before making final payment, require the contractor to provide this document confirming all subcontractors and suppliers have been paid.
- **Partial Release of Lien**: For progress payments, request partial lien releases.

### How to Protect Yourself

1. Always get a written contract before work begins
2. Never pay more than 10% upfront or $1,000 (whichever is less) without a written contract
3. Keep records of all payments (check or card - never cash)
4. Request lien releases with each payment
5. Verify the permit has been pulled before making the first payment

## Warranty Protections

### Manufacturer Warranty

- Most HVAC equipment comes with 5-10 year parts warranty
- Some manufacturers offer up to 12 years with product registration
- **Registration deadline**: Usually within 60-90 days of installation
- **Warranty invalidation risks**: Unlicensed installation, missing permit, improper sizing

### Contractor Workmanship Warranty

- Florida does not mandate a minimum workmanship warranty
- Reputable contractors provide 1-2 years on labor/workmanship
- Get the warranty terms in writing in your contract
- Warranty should cover: refrigerant charge, duct connections, electrical, drainage

### Implied Warranties

Under Florida law, even without an explicit warranty:

- **Implied warranty of merchantability**: The system must work as expected
- **Implied warranty of fitness**: If you specified requirements, the system must meet them
- **Statute of limitations**: 4 years for breach of contract, 15 years for latent defects in construction

## Contract Requirements

Florida law requires the following for home improvement contracts over $2,500:

- Written contract signed by both parties
- Contractor's license number on the contract
- Total price or method of determining price
- Approximate start and completion dates
- Description of work to be performed
- Payment schedule tied to completion milestones
- Permit responsibility stated (should always be the contractor)

### Three-Day Right to Cancel

For HVAC contracts signed at your home (not at the contractor's office):

- You have 3 business days to cancel without penalty
- Contractor must provide written notice of this right
- Cancellation must be in writing

## Dispute Resolution

### Step 1: Direct Communication

- Document the issue in writing (email preferred)
- Reference specific contract terms or code requirements
- Give the contractor a reasonable timeframe to respond

### Step 2: DBPR Complaint

If the contractor is unresponsive or refuses to fix issues:

1. File a complaint at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/intentions2.asp?chession=FLdbprGOC)
2. DBPR investigates complaints against licensed contractors
3. Penalties range from fines to license revocation

### Step 3: Construction Industry Licensing Board

- Handles formal hearings for serious violations
- Can order restitution, probation, or license revocation
- Process can take several months

### Step 4: Legal Action

- Small claims court for disputes under $8,000
- County court for disputes $8,001 - $50,000
- Circuit court for disputes over $50,000
- Many contracts include arbitration clauses (review before signing)

## Common Scams to Watch For

1. **"We don't need a permit"** - Every system replacement needs a permit. No exceptions.
2. **"Pay in full upfront"** - Legitimate contractors take deposits, not full payment before work.
3. **"Your system has a Freon leak, needs replacement"** - Get a second opinion. R-410A systems don't use Freon.
4. **"This price is only good today"** - High-pressure sales tactics. Good contractors don't rush you.
5. **"We'll match any quote"** - Extremely low bids often mean cut corners, undersized equipment, or no permit.
