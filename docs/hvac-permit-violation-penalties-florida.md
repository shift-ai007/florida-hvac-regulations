# HVAC Permit Violation Penalties in Florida

Most homeowners don't think about permits when replacing an AC system — the technician shows up, does the work, and charges for the unit. But Florida law requires permits for most HVAC work, and skipping them carries consequences that far exceed the permit fee itself. This guide covers the real cost of violating Florida's HVAC permitting requirements.

## What Work Requires a Permit?

Florida Building Code (FBC) Chapter 1, Section 105, and the Florida Mechanical Code (FMC) specify that permits are required for:

| Work Type | Permit Required? | Typical Fee Range |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Full system replacement (indoor + outdoor) | ✅ Always | $75-250 |
| New installation | ✅ Always | $100-350 |
| Ductwork modification or replacement | ✅ Always | $50-150 |
| Refrigerant circuit repair (replacing coil, compressor, TXV) | ✅ Required | $50-100 |
| Condenser-only replacement (lineset reuse) | ✅ Required | $75-150 |
| Electrical disconnect replacement | ✅ Required | $40-80 |
| Thermostat replacement (same type) | ❌ Not typically | N/A |
| Filter change, coil cleaning, condensate drain clearing | ❌ No | N/A |
| Minor refrigerant leak repair (less than 1lb with no component replacement) | ⚠️ Varies by county | $30-60 |

The general rule: if the work involves the refrigerant circuit, structural modification, or electrical hardwiring, it needs a permit. Cosmetic maintenance does not.

## Enforcement Mechanisms

### County Building Departments

Each county in South Florida enforces its own building code, though all follow the Florida Building Code as a baseline:

- **Miami-Dade** — The strictest in the state. Requires product approval (NOA) for equipment models, not just a permit. Miami-Dade has its own supplement to the FBC. Unpermitted work uncovered during a real estate transaction often results in a retroactive permit requirement with additional fees.
- **Broward** — Enforces per FBC with most municipalities (Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, Pembroke Pines) having their own inspection schedules. Broward County Environmental Code also regulates asbestos abatement for ductwork in older buildings.
- **Palm Beach** — Similar to Broward but with higher permit fees in some municipalities (West Palm Beach, Boca Raton). Palm Beach County requires contractor registration beyond the state license.

### How Violations Are Discovered

Violations rarely come from proactive sweeps. They surface through:

1. **Real estate transactions** — The most common trigger. When a home is sold, the buyer's title company or inspector may request permit records. Unpermitted work discovered during a sale can delay closing by weeks.
2. **Insurance claims** — If a new AC leaks and causes $15,000 in ceiling damage, the insurance adjuster often requests the permit record. If none exists, the claim may be denied for improper installation.
3. **Post-storm inspections** — After a major hurricane, building departments may require re-inspection of repairs. Unpermitted work flagged during recovery can stop a rebuild.
4. **Code compliance complaints** — A neighbor dispute or building management referral can trigger an inspection.
5. **Routine contractor audits** — Licensed contractors are occasionally audited, and their permit pull records are compared against their work orders. Discrepancies can trigger disciplinary action by the DBPR.

## Penalties and Consequences

### For Homeowners

**If you (or a contractor on your behalf) performed unpermitted work, the consequences include:**

- **Stop-work order** — The county can issue an immediate stop-work order, requiring you to halt any ongoing construction until permits are obtained. This can leave a home without AC for days while the paperwork processes.
- **Double or triple permit fees** — Many South Florida counties assess a penalty equal to or exceeding the original permit fee. Miami-Dade regularly charges 2× the base fee for late permits.
- **Certificate of Occupancy (CO) issues** — If the work affects the home's CO, selling or refinancing becomes difficult until it's resolved.
- **Forced corrective work** — An inspector may require you to open up walls or ceilings to verify proper installation, even if the new system works fine. This cost is entirely on the homeowner.
- **Liability for damages** — If unpermitted work causes a fire, flood, or structural issue, your homeowner's insurance may deny coverage, leaving you personally liable for all damages.

### For Contractors

Licensed contractors face much steeper consequences:

- **$500-$5,000 fines** — Per violation, per the DBPR disciplinary guidelines. Repeat offenses escalate quickly.
- **Probation or license suspension** — The Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) can place a contractor on probation, require additional continuing education, or suspend their license for 30 days to 1 year for knowingly performing work without permits.
- **Revocation** — For egregious or repeated offenses, permanent license revocation is possible.
- **Civil liability** — If unpermitted work fails and causes damage, the contractor is fully liable — and their insurance may deny the claim because the work was illegally performed.
- **Criminal charges** — In extreme cases (fraud, forgery of permit documents, or work that causes injury), Florida Statute 489.129 makes unlicensed or improper contracting a first-degree misdemeanor, with penalties up to 1 year in jail and $1,000 fines.

A single unpermitted installation can cost a contractor their entire career.

## The Permit Process (What It Actually Involves)

Many homeowners (and some contractors) skip permits because they assume it means days of waiting and hundreds in fees. Here's what a typical HVAC permit process looks like in South Florida:

1. **Application** — Contractor submits the permit application online (most counties now use e-permitting), including the scope of work, equipment specs, and contractor license number.
2. **Plan review** — For straight replacements, plan review is often waived. For new installations or substantial modifications, the county reviews the system design. This takes 1-5 business days.
3. **Permit issuance** — Fee is paid, permit number is issued. The contractor posts the permit at the job site.
4. **Inspections** — Required at key milestones:
   - Rough-in (before drywall is closed for new ductwork)
   - Final (system installed, operational, labeled)
5. **Certificate of Completion** — Final inspection passed, paperwork filed, job is legal.

Total cost: typically $100-350 for a straight AC replacement in most South Florida counties. Total time added: a few days at most, and the inspection can often be scheduled the same day as the final work.

| Scenario | Permit Route | No Permit Route |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| AC replacement in Miami-Dade | $150 permit fee + 2 days admin | $0 up front → $300 penalty + forced inspection + potential drywall opening ($500-1,500) if inspector finds concealment |
| Ductwork modification in Broward | $75 permit + 1-hour inspection | $0 → insurance claim denial ($5,000-15,000) if leak causes mold discovered later |
| New system install in Palm Beach | $200 permit + plan review | $0 → real estate sale delay ($200-400/day in holding costs) when buyer's attorney discovers no permit |

The economics strongly favor obtaining the permit.

## Verifying Permit Status

Before buying a home or approving a contractor's work:

1. **Check the county's online permit portal** — Most South Florida counties have searchable databases:
   - Miami-Dade: [permits.miamidade.gov](https://permits.miamidade.gov)
   - Broward: Search by municipality (most cities have their own portal)
   - Palm Beach: [discover.pbcgov.org](https://discover.pbcgov.org)

2. **Ask the contractor for the permit number** before work begins. A licensed contractor should provide it proactively.

3. **Check the DBPR license lookup** at [myfloridalicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com) to confirm the contractor holds a valid Certified or Registered HVAC contractor license. A contractor who asks you to "pull your own permit" is almost certainly unlicensed.
