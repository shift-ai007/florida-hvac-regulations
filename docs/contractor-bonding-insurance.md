# Florida HVAC Contractor Bonding & Insurance Requirements

Florida law requires licensed HVAC contractors to carry specific types and amounts of insurance coverage. These requirements protect homeowners, workers, and the contractor alike. This guide breaks down what coverage is mandatory, what's recommended, and how homeowners can verify a contractor has proper protection before hiring.

## Why This Matters for Homeowners

When you hire an HVAC contractor, you're allowing someone to work on one of your home's most expensive and essential systems. Proper insurance and bonding mean:

- **You're not liable** if a worker gets injured on your property
- **You're protected** if the contractor damages your home during installation
- **You have recourse** if the contractor fails to complete the job or performs substandard work
- **The contractor is legitimate** — carrying proper insurance demonstrates they operate above board

Hiring an uninsured contractor to save a few hundred dollars can cost tens of thousands if something goes wrong.

## Florida's Minimum Insurance Requirements for HVAC Contractors

Florida Administrative Code 61G15-22.001 sets the minimum insurance requirements for certified and registered HVAC contractors.

### General Liability Insurance
| Requirement | Minimum Amount |
|-------------|----------------|
| General liability (per occurrence) | $100,000 |
| General liability (aggregate) | $200,000 |
| Property damage | $100,000 |
| Personal injury | $100,000 |

Many homeowner associations, property managers, and commercial clients require significantly higher limits — typically $1 million to $2 million aggregate.

### Workers' Compensation Insurance
Florida law requires workers' compensation coverage if the contractor has:
- **Four or more employees** (full-time or part-time) in the construction industry
- **One or more employees** if the contractor is a corporation or LLC

Sole proprietors and single-member LLCs with no employees are not required to carry workers' comp for themselves, but they may choose to. **Homeowners should verify workers' comp exists even for small crews** — if a subcontractor's employee is injured on your property without coverage, the medical bills could fall on the homeowner.

### Business Auto Liability
If the contractor operates company vehicles, Florida Financial Responsibility limits apply:
- $10,000 property damage per accident (minimum)
- $10,000 personal injury protection per person
- Higher limits recommended for contractors hauling equipment

Many contractors carry commercial auto policies with $500,000 to $1 million combined single limits.

## Surety Bonding Requirements

### State License Bond
Florida does NOT require a general surety bond for HVAC contractors at the state level. Instead, the state's recovery fund provides a layer of consumer protection (discussed below).

### Local Municipality Bonds
Some Florida municipalities and counties require HVAC contractors to post additional bonds:
- **Miami-Dade County** requires a $5,000 performance bond for certain HVAC permits
- **Broward County** municipalities (Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, Pembroke Pines) may require bonds for specific commercial projects
- **City of Miami** requires general contractor bonds; HVAC contractors may need one for work over $5,000

Always check local requirements with the building department where the work will be performed.

### Homeowners' Construction Recovery Fund

Florida's most important consumer protection mechanism is the **Homeowners' Construction Recovery Fund**, administered by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR).

**How it works:**
1. Every licensed contractor (including HVAC) pays into the fund at initial licensing and renewal
2. If a contractor performs substandard work, abandons a project, or fails to complete a job, the homeowner can file a claim
3. The fund pays up to **$25,000** per claim (with a $30,000 maximum per contractor)
4. The contractor's license is suspended until the fund is repaid

**Limitations:**
- Only applies to work performed on the homeowner's primary residence
- Claims must be filed within 2 years of discovering the violation
- The homeowner must first attempt to resolve the issue directly with the contractor
- The fund does not cover breach of contract (poor workmanship claims only)
- Judgments or settlements from other parties reduce the fund claim amount

This fund is not insurance — it's a safety net of last resort. It can take 6-18 months to receive payment from the fund.

## Recommended Coverage Beyond Minimums

Most reputable HVAC contractors in South Florida carry coverage well above state minimums. Minimum coverage ($100K/$200K) is inadequate for most real-world scenarios.

| Scenario | Minimum Coverage Risk | Recommended Coverage |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| AC unit falls through ceiling during installation | $100K property damage may not cover full damage + cleanup | $500K-$1M |
| Ductwork fire caused by improper installation | $200K aggregate exhausted by first claim | $1M-$2M |
| Customer slips on wet floor during service | Medical bills exceed $100K per occurrence | $1M-$2M |
| Homeowner association requires proof | Most HOAs require $1M minimum | $1M-$2M |

**What to ask for:** Request a certificate of insurance (COI) dated within the last 90 days showing:
- General liability: $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate
- Workers' compensation: coverage for all employees and subcontractors
- Commercial auto: $500,000 combined single limit or higher
- Additional insured endorsement naming you or your property (for larger projects)

## How to Verify a Contractor's Insurance

### Step 1: Get the Certificate of Insurance (COI)
Ask the contractor to email you a current COI. It should list:
- The insurance company's name and NAIC number
- Policy number and effective dates (verify the policy hasn't expired)
- Coverage types and limits
- The contractor's business name exactly as it appears on their state license

### Step 2: Call the Insurance Agency
Most COIs include the agent's contact information. Call to verify:
- The policy is active (not canceled or non-renewed)
- Coverage limits match what was promised
- Workers' comp is listed if the contractor has employees

### Step 3: Verify the License
Check the contractor's license status through the DBPR online portal at myfloridalicense.com. A valid license should be in "Active" status with no disciplinary actions or suspensions.

### Step 4: Check for Claims History (For Major Projects)
For projects over $10,000, consider asking for a loss-run report from the contractor's insurance carrier. This shows recent claims. While contractors may decline to share this, a willingness to provide it signals transparency.

## Red Flags in Insurance and Bonding

| Red Flag | What It Means |
|----------|---------------|
| "I'm fully insured but don't have a COI handy" | Likely uninsured or underinsured |
| Expired policy dates on the COI | Policy may have lapsed |
| Different business name on COI vs. license | May be using another company's insurance |
| "I'm a sole proprietor, so I don't need workers' comp" | True — but only if they truly work alone |
| "Just get a handyman, no permit needed" | Handyman exemption is limited to $500 in FL |
| "I'll put you on my insurance for the day" | Not how commercial insurance works |
| Cash-only payment terms | Common among unlicensed operators |

## What Happens If a Contractor Is Uninsured?

### Scenario A: Worker Injury on Your Property
If an uninsured HVAC technician is injured at your home, your homeowner's insurance may cover medical payments (typically $1,000-$5,000), but beyond that, you could be personally liable. Medical bills for a serious injury (fall from a ladder, electrical shock) can easily exceed $100,000.

Florida's workers' compensation law places the burden on the contractor, but if the contractor has no workers' comp and no assets to cover the claim, the injured worker's recourse may be against the homeowner.

### Scenario B: Property Damage During Installation
If an uninsured contractor drops a 200-pound condenser unit through your wall or causes an electrical fire, your homeowner's insurance may cover the damage — but you'll pay the deductible ($1,000-$5,000) and face increased premiums. If homeowner's insurance denies the claim (some policies exclude contractor-caused damage), you bear the full cost.

### Scenario C: Substandard Work or Abandonment
Without a licensed contractor, you cannot access the Homeowners' Construction Recovery Fund. Your only recourse is small claims court (limited to $8,000) or civil court — both time-consuming and uncertain.
