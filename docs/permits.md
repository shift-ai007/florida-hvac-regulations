# When You Need an HVAC Permit in Florida

Florida requires permits for most HVAC work. Performing work without a permit can result in fines, failed insurance claims, and complications when selling your home.

## Permit Required

The following HVAC jobs **always** require a building permit in Florida:

### New Installations
- Installing a new central AC system (split or package unit)
- Installing a new heat pump system
- Mini-split system installation (any number of zones)
- New ductwork installation in unconditioned spaces

### Replacements
- Replacing an outdoor condenser unit
- Replacing an indoor air handler
- Replacing both components (full system changeout)
- Replacing a furnace or heat pump
- Switching system types (e.g., heat pump to straight cool, or vice versa)

### Major Modifications
- Changing system capacity (upsizing or downsizing tonnage)
- Rerouting or adding duct runs
- Adding a zone to an existing system
- Relocating indoor or outdoor equipment
- Adding or relocating a thermostat with new wiring runs
- Converting from window units to central air

### Commercial
- All commercial HVAC installations and replacements
- Rooftop unit installations
- Chiller or cooling tower work
- Variable refrigerant flow (VRF) system installations

## Permit NOT Usually Required

These minor repairs and maintenance tasks typically do not require a permit:

- Replacing a thermostat (same location, same wiring)
- Cleaning or replacing air filters
- Cleaning evaporator and condenser coils
- Replacing a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor
- Recharging refrigerant (with existing charge port)
- Unclogging a condensate drain line
- Replacing a condensate pump
- Insulating exposed ductwork (repair, not new install)
- Replacing a disconnect or whip (electrical permit may apply)

**Note**: Permit requirements vary by county. When in doubt, call your local building department. It's always better to pull a permit and not need one than to skip it and face consequences.

## County-Specific Nuances

### Miami-Dade County
- Uses Miami-Dade County-specific product approvals for impact-related components
- Online permit system: [Miami-Dade iBuild](https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/)
- Hurricane tie-down requirements for outdoor units
- Additional wind load calculations for rooftop equipment

### Broward County
- Electronic permitting via [Broward County ePermits](https://www.broward.org/Planning/Pages/default.aspx)
- Same-day inspection scheduling available for HVAC
- Requires FPL load calculation approval for system upsizing

### Palm Beach County
- Permits through [Palm Beach County EZPermit](https://www.pbcgov.org/pzb/building-division/)
- Green building incentives for high-efficiency HVAC installations
- Additional tree setback requirements for condenser placement

## What Happens Without a Permit

If HVAC work is done without a required permit:

1. **Discovery**: Usually found during home sale inspection or insurance claim
2. **Stop work order**: Building department can halt ongoing unpermitted work
3. **After-the-fact permit**: You'll need to apply for one (costs 2-4x normal permit fees)
4. **Inspection**: The work must be inspected, which may require opening walls/ceilings
5. **Rework**: If it doesn't meet code, you pay to bring it into compliance
6. **Insurance**: Claims can be denied for unpermitted HVAC work
7. **Home sale**: Title companies and buyers may require permit resolution before closing

## Permit Costs (Typical Ranges)

| Work Type | Permit Fee Range |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Residential AC replacement (like-for-like) | $75 - $200 |
| New residential installation | $150 - $400 |
| System with ductwork modification | $200 - $500 |
| Commercial HVAC | $300 - $1,000+ |
| After-the-fact permit | 2x - 4x normal fee |

*Fees vary by county. Contact your local building department for current schedules.*

## The Contractor's Responsibility

In Florida, the **licensed contractor** is responsible for:
- Pulling the permit before work begins
- Scheduling inspections
- Ensuring the installation passes inspection
- Closing the permit after final inspection

Homeowners should verify that the permit was pulled by checking their county's online permit portal. Never let a contractor tell you "we don't need a permit for this" on a system replacement.
