# Florida HVAC Sales Tax Guide — What's Taxable and What's Not

Florida sales tax on HVAC work is more nuanced than most homeowners (and many contractors) realize. Misclassifying a job can result in tax liability on audit, and for homeowners, knowing the rules can save 6-7% on a major installation.

## The Basic Rule

Florida imposes sales tax (6% state + applicable county surtax) on the **sale of tangible personal property**, including HVAC equipment and parts. Labor charges are taxable when they are part of a repair that involves installing taxable parts. The distinction matters for larger jobs.

## Residential HVAC: Repair vs. Capital Improvement

### Repairs (Taxable)

A *repair* maintains, replaces, or restores existing equipment. Sales tax applies to the entire charge — parts and labor:

- Replacing a failed capacitor or contactor
- Repairing a refrigerant leak and recharging the system
- Replacing a blower motor or fan
- Replacing a condenser fan motor
- Annual maintenance with replacement parts (filters are taxable if sold separately)

### Capital Improvements (Not Taxable — with conditions)

Under Florida Administrative Code Rule 12A-1.051, a *capital improvement* is work that substantially extends the life of the property, increases its value, or adapts it to a new use. For HVAC:

- **Complete system replacement (indoor + outdoor units)** is generally a non-taxable capital improvement if the contractor provides a properly executed certificate
- **New installation** in a home that did not previously have central AC is a capital improvement
- **Ductwork replacement** as part of a system overhaul is treated as attached to real property

**The contractor must issue Form DR-123 (Certificate of Capital Improvement) to the homeowner.** Without it, the transaction is presumed taxable on audit.

### Gray Areas

| Scenario | Tax Treatment | Reasoning |
|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Replace outdoor unit, keep existing air handler | Taxable repair (usually) | Not a complete system replacement |
| Replace indoor coil + outdoor unit, keep existing furnace | Gray area | Argue either way — depends on scope |
| Replace entire split system (condenser, air handler/furnace, coil, line set) | Capital improvement | Complete functional replacement |
| Add a mini-split to one room | Capital improvement | New installation |
| Duct replacement only (no equipment) | Capital improvement | Real property improvement |

## Commercial HVAC

Commercial work follows the same legal framework but has additional complications:

- **Real property contracts** — Large commercial installations (chillers, VRF systems, built-up systems) are generally non-taxable as real property improvements when the equipment is permanently affixed
- **Service contracts** — Annual maintenance agreements are taxable as a service unless they cover only inspection/monitoring with no parts
- **Tenant improvements** — The tax treatment follows who owns the equipment. Landlord-owned = real property. Tenant-owned equipment that remains with the tenant = potentially taxable as personal property

## County Surtax Rates (2026)

Florida's state base rate is 6%, but most counties add a discretionary sales surtax:

| County | Total Rate | Surtax |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Miami-Dade | 7% | 1% |
| Broward | 7% | 1% |
| Palm Beach | 7% | 1% |
| Hillsborough | 7.5% | 1.5% |
| Orange | 6.5% | 0.5% |
| Duval | 7.5% | 1.5% |

For South Florida homeowners, the 1% surtax on a $12,000 system replacement is $120 — worth getting the capital improvement classification right.

## Contractor Responsibilities

Florida-licensed HVAC contractors are required to:

1. Register with the Florida Department of Revenue and collect/report sales tax
2. Determine the taxability of each job at the time of contracting
3. Provide a DR-123 certificate to the customer for capital improvements
4. Maintain records distinguishing taxable repairs from non-taxable improvements
5. Pay use tax on any equipment or materials purchased tax-free that are later used in a taxable repair

A contractor who fails to collect tax on a taxable repair is still liable for the tax plus penalties. This liability is personal — it survives closing the business.

## For Homeowners: What You Should Know

- **Ask for a DR-123 certificate** on any complete system replacement. If the contractor won't provide one, ask why they're classifying it as a repair.
- **Get the sales tax treatment on the proposal** — a written statement protects you if the auditor disagrees later
- **Manufacturer rebates don't change taxability** — the tax applies to the price you pay, not the pre-rebate price
- **Separately stated charges** — If the invoice separates equipment cost from labor, the classification (repair vs. capital improvement) still applies to the entire contract. You can't tax the parts and exempt the labor on the same job.

## Energy-Efficient Equipment Tax Holiday

Florida occassionally enacts sales tax holidays on ENERGY STAR appliances and equipment. When active, these may exempt qualifying HVAC equipment purchases. Check the Florida Department of Revenue website for current tax holiday periods — they're typically announced in spring before cooling season.

## Disclaimer

This is a practical reference, not formal tax advice. Florida sales tax law is interpreted through statutes, administrative code, and Department of Revenue technical advisements. For specific transactions, consult a Florida-licensed CPA or tax attorney familiar with construction industry taxation.
