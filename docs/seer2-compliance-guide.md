# SEER2 Compliance Guide for South Florida Homeowners

## What Changed on January 1, 2023

The Department of Energy's new efficiency standards took effect January 1, 2023, replacing the old SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) rating with SEER2. This wasn't just a labeling change — it introduced stricter testing procedures that reflect real-world operating conditions more accurately.

Under the new standard:

- **Southern states (including Florida)**: New residential AC units must meet a minimum of **15.0 SEER2**
- **Northern states**: Minimum **14.3 SEER2**
- **Heat pumps**: Minimum **14.3 SEER2** nationwide

A unit that rated 14 SEER under the old system typically rates about 13.4-13.8 SEER2 under the new testing protocol. The difference reflects more realistic external static pressure assumptions (0.5 in. w.c. vs the old 0.1-0.3 in. w.c. range), which better represents what happens when a unit is connected to actual ductwork.

## Why This Matters in South Florida

South Florida's air conditioners run 2,500-3,500 hours annually — roughly 3-4 times more than the national average. This makes efficiency ratings disproportionately important here compared to most of the country.

### The Dollar Math

Moving from a 14 SEER (pre-2023) system to a 16 SEER2 system in a 2,000 sq ft Miami home with average electricity rates ($0.14/kWh):

| Metric | Old 14 SEER | New 16 SEER2 | Annual Difference |
|--------|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Annual kWh | ~8,400 | ~7,100 | ~1,300 kWh saved |
| Annual cost | ~$1,176 | ~$994 | ~$182 saved |
| 15-year cost | ~$17,640 | ~$14,910 | ~$2,730 saved |

This doesn't account for FPL's expected rate increases, which would widen the gap further.

## What You Can Still Buy

Distributors and contractors can still sell and install units manufactured before January 1, 2023 that don't meet SEER2 minimums. This is commonly called "sell-through" inventory. However:

- **Warranty coverage may differ** — some manufacturers limit warranty terms on discontinued models
- **Parts availability** narrows as non-compliant models phase out
- **Future serviceability** becomes a concern 5-8 years out

If a contractor quotes you a price that seems unusually low, ask whether the unit meets current SEER2 minimums. A pre-2023 unit might save you $500-800 upfront but cost $2,000+ in additional electricity over its service life.

## Florida Building Code Overlay

Florida's Building Code (8th Edition, 2023) incorporates these federal standards by reference through Chapter 13 (Energy Efficiency). Section R403 requires:

1. **Duct leakage testing**: Maximum 4 cfm per 100 sq ft of conditioned floor area at 25 Pa
2. **Refrigerant charge verification**: Must be documented at installation
3. **Airflow verification**: Must meet manufacturer specifications (±15%)

These aren't optional recommendations — they're code requirements. A permitted installation in Miami-Dade, Broward, or Palm Beach County must include documentation of all three.

## The Tax Credit Angle

The Inflation Reduction Act's 25C tax credit (extended through 2032) covers:

- **30% of cost up to $600** for qualifying AC units meeting Energy Star Most Efficient criteria
- **30% up to $2,000** for qualifying heat pumps

For South Florida homeowners, a 17+ SEER2 unit that meets Energy Star Most Efficient criteria qualifies for the $600 credit, effectively reducing the premium for high-efficiency equipment.

## Practical Recommendations

1. **If replacing before 2028**: Buy SEER2-compliant. The small premium over sell-through inventory is recovered through operating savings within 3-5 years in South Florida's climate.

2. **If your unit is 8-12 years old and still functional**: Start planning now. Efficiency standards may tighten again (DOE reviews standards every 6 years), and prices typically adjust upward with each new tier.

3. **Get the duct leakage test**: Florida code requires it, but many contractors skip it on replacement jobs. If your ducts leak at 15% (common in pre-2010 South Florida homes), fixing them can improve system efficiency more than buying a higher-SEER unit.

4. **Match indoor and outdoor units**: Installing a SEER2-rated outdoor unit with an older indoor coil can reduce efficiency by 10-20% and may void the manufacturer's warranty.
