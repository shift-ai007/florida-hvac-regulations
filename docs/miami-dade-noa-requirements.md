# Miami-Dade County NOA (Notice of Acceptance) — What HVAC Contractors and Homeowners Need to Know

Miami-Dade County maintains a unique product approval system that is stricter than the Florida Building Code baseline. If you're installing or replacing HVAC equipment in Miami-Dade, understanding NOA requirements is essential — install non-approved equipment and you'll fail inspection.

## What Is an NOA?

A **Notice of Acceptance (NOA)** is Miami-Dade County's certification that a specific building product — including HVAC equipment, mounting systems, and roofing penetrations — has been tested and approved for use within the county.

The NOA system exists because Miami-Dade is in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ), which extends through Miami-Dade and Broward counties. Building products in this zone must pass more rigorous wind-load, impact, and water-penetration testing than anywhere else in Florida.

## What Needs an NOA?

For HVAC installations, NOA approval is required for:

| Component | Why It Needs NOA |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Outdoor condenser mounting stands/platforms** | Must withstand 175+ mph wind loads and stay anchored to the pad |
| **Roof-mounted HVAC equipment** | Wind uplift forces are highest at roof level; mounting must be engineered and approved |
| **Roof penetrations** (curbs, flashings, duct boots) | Must pass water-penetration testing at hurricane wind-driven rain pressures |
| **Wall penetrations** (line sets passing through exterior walls) | Must be sealed, flashed, or sleeved per approved methods |
| **Equipment screens and enclosures** | Must not become airborne debris during a hurricane |

Indoor equipment (air handlers, furnaces) does not require NOA. Standard condensing units placed on a ground-level concrete pad typically don't need NOA for the unit itself — but all roof-penetrating line sets, curbs, and flashings do.

## How to Verify NOA Status

Miami-Dade maintains a public searchable database at its Building Code Compliance Office website:

1. Search by manufacturer name or product category
2. Verify the NOA number is active (not expired or revoked)
3. Confirm the NOA covers the specific model being installed
4. Print or save the NOA document — inspectors may ask for it

An NOA number on a competitor's quote means nothing until you verify it yourself. Expired NOAs, wrong-model-number NOAs, and "pending" NOAs are common issues that only surface at inspection.

## The Broward County Difference

Broward County is also in the HVHZ but does **not** maintain its own NOA system. Instead, Broward accepts:

- Miami-Dade NOAs
- Florida Building Commission product approvals (state-level FL #
- Engineer-signed calculations for site-specific installations

In practice, this means Miami-Dade NOA approval is the gold standard. If a product has a Miami-Dade NOA, it's accepted everywhere in South Florida.

## NOA vs State Product Approval (FL #)

There are two parallel product approval paths in Florida:

| | Miami-Dade NOA | Florida State Approval (FL #) |
|---|---|---|
| **Issuing authority** | Miami-Dade Board of Rules and Appeals | Florida Building Commission |
| **Jurisdiction** | Miami-Dade County only | Statewide |
| **Testing standard** | HVHZ-specific (more stringent) | Florida Building Code baseline |
| **Mutual acceptance** | Accepted statewide by statute | Not automatically accepted in Miami-Dade without additional documentation |

Florida Statute 553.842 requires that Miami-Dade NOAs be accepted statewide for products within their scope. But the reverse is not automatic — a state FL # alone does not guarantee acceptance in Miami-Dade.

## The Cost of Getting It Wrong

Installing non-NOA-compliant equipment in Miami-Dade:

1. **Failed inspection** — The inspector can't sign off
2. **Replacement at owner expense** — Non-compliant equipment must be removed and replaced
3. **Permit hold** — The permit stays open, and the system can't legally operate
4. **Insurance implications** — Some carriers require documentation that HVAC installations meet local code. A failed inspection can complicate a future claim.

For a typical 3-ton residential system, rectifying an NOA issue costs $800-2,500 in labor plus any equipment replacement — all avoidable with a pre-install NOA check.

## Tips for Homeowners

- Ask your contractor: "Is this equipment NOA-approved for Miami-Dade County?"
- Get the NOA number in writing on the proposal
- If you live in a condo or HOA, the association may have additional equipment requirements beyond the NOA
- For roof-mounted equipment (townhomes, condos, commercial buildings), NOA compliance is absolutely non-negotiable

## Where to Find Current Information

The Miami-Dade County Building Code Compliance Office maintains the official NOA database. Regulations are updated after each Florida Building Code revision cycle (every 3 years). HVAC contractors should check NOA status as part of every permit application.
